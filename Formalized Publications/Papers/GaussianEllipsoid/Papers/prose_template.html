The Strict Threshold for Gaussian Ellipsoid Fitting

Aykut Arslan
with Muse Spark

Mathematical review by
Babak Modami, Alexander Roitershtein, Mark Sepanski, and Grigory Sokolov

Abstract.

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) be independent \(N(0,I_d)\) vectors. We study whether there exists a positive semidefinite matrix \(S\) such that \(x_i^T S x_i=d\) for every \(1\le i\le n.\) We prove a sharp threshold at \(n\sim d^2/4\).

If \(\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{n(d)}{d^2}<\frac14,\) then with probability tending to one such an \(S\) exists, with \(S\) in fact positive definite.

If \(\liminf_{d\to\infty}\frac{n(d)}{d^2}>\frac14,\) then with probability tending to one no such \(S\) exists. We make no claim when \(n(d)/d^2\to1/4\).

1 Introduction and statement of the main result

Human

We consider the following problem. Given \(n\) random points in \(\mathbb R^d\), when do they lie on the boundary of an ellipsoid? For small \(n\) and a standard Gaussian setting such an event has a high likelihood, while for sufficiently large \(n\) it is unlikely to occur. The main regime of interest is therefore near the phase transition, which turns out to be \(n\asymp d^2\) [3, 7, 11].

We study the random setting in which \[x_1,\ldots,x_n\sim N(0,I_d)\] are independent. Since \(\|x_i\|_2^2\) is typically close to \(d\), the sphere already gives an approximate fit. The question is whether it can be deformed into a centered ellipsoid so that all of the points lie exactly on its boundary.

Saunderson, Parrilo, and Willsky [11] conjectured that this problem has a sharp transition at \(n=d^2/4\). More precisely, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), they predicted feasibility of such an ellipsoid with high probability when \[n\le\left(\frac14-\varepsilon\right)d^2,\] and infeasibility with high probability when \[n\ge\left(\frac14+\varepsilon\right)d^2.\]

The value \(1/4\) comes from the geometry of the positive semidefinite cone. One way to measure the size of this cone is to take a random Gaussian symmetric matrix, project it onto the cone, and compute the expected squared size of that projection. For \(d\times d\) matrices, this quantity is \(d(d+1)/4\), making \(1/4\) the natural candidate for the asymptotic threshold.

Earlier work established feasibility in increasingly large ranges of \(n\), eventually reaching \(n\le cd^2\) for a sufficiently small constant \(c>0\). Maillard and Kunisky [7] predicted the sharp transition at \(1/4\) using the non-rigorous replica method of statistical physics. Bandeira and Maillard [3] later proved the \(1/4\) threshold for an approximate version of the ellipsoid-fitting problem. The corresponding exact fitting problem remained open.

We prove the exact threshold with fixed margin. No assertion is made for the critical case when \(n/d^2\to1/4\).

Theorem 1.1 (Strict-threshold theorem).

Let \(n=n(d)\) be any sequence of positive integers. For each \(d\), let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) be independent \(N(0,I_d)\) vectors and define \[\mathcal F_d=\{S\succeq0:S^\top = S \text{~and~} x_i^TSx_i=d\text{ for all }1\le i\le n\}.\] Then, the following statements hold.

  1. If \(\limsup_{d\to\infty}n/d^2<1/4\), then \[\mathbb P\bigl(\text{there exists }S\succ0 \text{ with }x_i^TSx_i=d \text{ for every } i\bigr)\longrightarrow1.\]

  2. If \(\liminf_{d\to\infty}n/d^2>1/4\), then \(\mathbb P(\mathcal F_d\ne\varnothing)\to0\).

Concurrent work

Human

While completing this work, we became aware of the concurrent work of Misiakiewicz and Wen [9], posted on August 10, 2026.

The two works prove the same sharp threshold, but use different proof architectures. Misiakiewicz and Wen build directly on the Gaussian-equivalence framework of Bandeira–Maillard. Below the threshold, they use a head–tail decomposition of the dual vector, with exact treatment of the sparse head and Gaussian comparison for the low-influence tail. Above the threshold, they combine a low-rank/diffuse decomposition with conditional Gaussianization and a projected Gordon escape argument.

Our proof follows a different route. Below the threshold, we begin with the approximate fitting result of Bandeira–Maillard and perturb it to an exact fit using a spectrally constrained robust-loss construction, a sparse/diffuse decomposition of the coefficient vector, and a dyadic multiscale estimate. Above the threshold, we also use Gordon’s Gaussian min–max theorem, but in a different role. We first establish the Gaussian margin and then transfer it to centered rank-one measurements through a leave-one-row Gibbs interpolation, in the spirit of rigorous cavity methods [1, 12]. A Grassmann-net conditioning argument and a finite large-eigenvalue decomposition then extend the diffuse estimate to the full positive semidefinite cone.

3 Previous work: Approximate-fitting theorem

Human

We use the groundbreaking approximate-fitting theorem of Bandeira and Maillard [3, Theorem 1.4] which asserts that if \(\limsup n/d^2<1/4\), then there are constants \[0<\lambda_-\le\lambda_+<\infty\] such that, for every \(1\le r<4/3\) and \(n=n(d)\), \[\min_{\lambda_-I\preceq S\preceq\lambda_+I} \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \left|\sqrt d\left(\frac{x_i^TSx_i}{d}-1\right) \right|^r\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0 \text{ as $d \to\infty$.}\] The residual inside the absolute value is exactly \(\operatorname{res}_{i}(S)\). Taking \(r=1\) gives a measurable approximate solution \(\widetilde S_d\) in the fixed spectral interval \[\mathcal S_d= \{S:\lambda_-I\preceq S\preceq\lambda_+I\}\] such that \[\frac1n\sum_i|\operatorname{res}_{i}(\widetilde S_d)| \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.\] The comparator may depend on the full sample. It is used only in pathwise objective comparisons, so no independence is required.

5.1 Capped determinant moments

We begin with the negative determinant estimate used in the bilinear small-ball argument. The proof includes the extremization over all admissible coefficient vectors.

Lemma 5.1 (Capped simplex extremizer).

Let \(N\ge1\), let \(0<a<1\), and assume that \[\mathcal W_{N,a}=\left\{w\in[0,a]^N:\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i=1\right\}\] is nonempty. Put \[k=\lfloor a^{-1}\rfloor\] and \[\rho=1-ka.\] Thus \(0\le\rho<a\). After adding or deleting zero coordinates, define \[w^*=(a,\ldots,a,\rho,0,\ldots,0),\] where \(a\) occurs \(k\) times and the coordinate \(\rho\) is omitted when it is zero. Feasibility of \(\mathcal W_{N,a}\) ensures that this vector has at most \(N\) nonzero coordinates. Let \(g_1,\ldots,g_N\) be independent \(N(0,I_r)\) vectors. For every \(s>0\), \[\mathbb E\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^Nw_ig_ig_i^T\right)^{-s} \le \mathbb E\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i^*g_ig_i^T\right)^{-s}\] for every \(w\in\mathcal W_{N,a}\), where both sides are understood as extended nonnegative expectations.

Proof

For \(\varepsilon>0\), define \[\Phi_\varepsilon(w)= \mathbb E\det\left(\varepsilon I_r+\sum_{i=1}^Nw_ig_ig_i^T\right)^{-s}.\] The map \[A\longmapsto\det(A)^{-s}=\exp[-s\log\det A]\] is convex on the positive definite cone. Indeed, \(-\log\det A\) is convex, and the exponential is convex and increasing. Hence \(\Phi_\varepsilon\) is convex in \(w\). It is also invariant under coordinate permutations because the \(g_i\) are identically distributed.

We next verify the required majorization directly. Arrange the coordinates of \(w\) in decreasing order. For every \(1\le j\le N\), \[\sum_{i=1}^jw_i\le\min\{ja,1\}.\] The right side is the sum of the first \(j\) coordinates of \(w^*\). Thus \(w^*\) majorizes \(w\). By the finite-dimensional majorization theorem, there is a doubly stochastic matrix \(D\) such that \(w=Dw^*\). By the Birkhoff theorem, \(D\) is a convex combination of permutation matrices. Convexity and permutation invariance therefore give \[\Phi_\varepsilon(w)\le\Phi_\varepsilon(w^*).\] As \(\varepsilon\) decreases to zero, both integrands increase pointwise. Monotone convergence proves the asserted extended-valued inequality.

QED.

Lemma 5.2 (Real Wishart negative moment).

Let \(G\) be an \(r\) by \(k\) real matrix with independent \(N(0,1)\) entries and let \(W=GG^T\). If \(s>0\) and \[k>r+2s-1,\] then \[\mathbb E\det(W)^{-s} =2^{-rs}\prod_{j=1}^r \frac{\Gamma((k-j+1)/2-s)}{\Gamma((k-j+1)/2)}.\] If \(k\le r+2s-1\), the negative moment is infinite.

Proof

If \(k<r\), then \(\operatorname{rank}(W)=\operatorname{rank}(G)\leq k<r\), so \(\det(W)=0\) almost surely and \(\mathbb E\det(W)^{-s}=+\infty\) for every \(s>0\). Assume henceforth \(k\geq r\).

Apply Gram-Schmidt to the rows of \(G\). For \(1\leq j\leq r\leq k\), the squared length of the component of row \(j\) orthogonal to the preceding \(j-1\) rows has a chi-square law with \(k-j+1\) degrees of freedom. Gaussian orthogonal invariance also makes these squared lengths independent. Consequently, \[\det(W)\stackrel{d}=\prod_{j=1}^rX_j,\] where the \(X_j\) are independent and \(X_j\) has a chi-square law with \(k-j+1\) degrees of freedom.

If \(X\) has a chi-square law with \(m\) degrees of freedom, direct integration of its density gives \[\mathbb EX^{-s} =\frac1{2^{m/2}\Gamma(m/2)} \int_0^\infty x^{m/2-s-1}e^{-x/2}dx =2^{-s}\frac{\Gamma(m/2-s)}{\Gamma(m/2)}.\] This integral is finite exactly when \(m>2s\). Multiplying the \(r\) independent moments proves the formula and the finiteness condition.

QED.

Proposition 5.3 (Uniform capped determinant moment).

Fix \(0<\theta<1/\sqrt2\). There is \(C_\theta<\infty\) such that the following holds for every \(r,N\ge1\). Let \(g_1,\ldots,g_N\) be independent \(N(0,I_r)\) vectors and let \(z\in\mathbb R^N\) satisfy \[\|z\|_2=1\] and \[\sqrt r\|z\|_\infty\le\theta.\] Then, with \[W_z=\sum_{i=1}^Nz_i^2g_ig_i^T,\] one has \[\mathbb E\det(W_z)^{-r/2}\le C_\theta^{r^2}.\] One may take \[C_\theta=(1-2\theta^2)^{-1/2}\] for \(r\ge2\), after increasing the constant to cover \(r=1\).

Proof

Set \[a=\frac{\theta^2}{r}, k=\left\lfloor\frac r{\theta^2}\right\rfloor, \rho=1-ka.\] The weights \(w_i=z_i^2\) belong to \(\mathcal W_{N,a}\). By Lemma 5.1, their negative determinant moment is at most the moment at the capped vector \(w^*\). For independent Gaussian vectors \(g_1,\ldots,g_{k+1}\), \[\sum_iw_i^*g_ig_i^T =a\sum_{i=1}^kg_ig_i^T+\rho g_{k+1}g_{k+1}^T \succeq a\sum_{i=1}^kg_ig_i^T.\] When \(\rho=0\), the last rank-one term is absent. Monotonicity of the determinant on the positive definite cone gives \[\mathbb E\det(W_z)^{-r/2} \le a^{-r^2/2}\mathbb E\det(W_{r,k})^{-r/2},\] where \(W_{r,k}\) is a real Wishart matrix with \(k\) degrees of freedom.

Put \[\delta_\theta=\theta^{-2}-2>0.\] The floor in the definition of \(k\) gives \[k\ge\frac r{\theta^2}-1\] and therefore \[k-2r+1\ge\delta_\theta r>0.\] In particular, \(k>2r-1\), so Lemma 5.2 applies with \(s=r/2\). It gives \[\mathbb E\det(W_{r,k})^{-r/2} =2^{-r^2/2}\prod_{j=1}^r \frac{\Gamma(y_j)}{\Gamma(y_j+r/2)},\] where \[y_j=\frac{k-j+1-r}{2}.\] The same floor estimate yields \[y_j\ge\frac{k-2r+1}{2}\ge\frac{\delta_\theta r}{2}.\]

Assume first that \(r\ge2\). If \(X\) has the gamma distribution with shape \(y_j\) and unit scale, then \[\frac{\Gamma(y_j+r/2)}{\Gamma(y_j)}=\mathbb EX^{r/2}.\] Since \(r/2\ge1\), Jensen’s inequality gives \[\mathbb EX^{r/2}\ge(\mathbb EX)^{r/2}=y_j^{r/2}.\] Thus \[\frac{\Gamma(y_j)}{\Gamma(y_j+r/2)} \le y_j^{-r/2} \le \left(\frac2{\delta_\theta r}\right)^{r/2}.\] Multiplication over \(j\) now gives \[\mathbb E\det(W_z)^{-r/2} \le \left(\frac1{2a}\right)^{r^2/2} \left(\frac2{\delta_\theta r}\right)^{r^2/2} = \left(\frac1{\theta^2\delta_\theta}\right)^{r^2/2} = (1-2\theta^2)^{-r^2/2}.\] This calculation includes the floor in \(k\) and does not use an asymptotic replacement of \(ak\) by one.

For \(r=1\), the exact expression from Lemma 5.2 is finite because \(k=\lfloor\theta^{-2}\rfloor\ge2\). It is a finite number depending only on \(\theta\). Increasing \(C_\theta\) to cover this one case completes the proof.

QED.

5.2 Arbitrary-center Frobenius small balls

Proposition 5.4 (Determinant small ball).

Fix \(0<\theta<1/\sqrt2\). There is \(C_\theta<\infty\) such that the following holds. Let \(g_i,k_i\) be mutually independent \(N(0,I_r)\) vectors, let \(z\in\mathbb R^N\) satisfy \[\|z\|_2=1\] and \[\sqrt r\|z\|_\infty\le\theta,\] and put \[Y_z=\frac1{\sqrt r}\sum_{i=1}^Nz_ig_ik_i^T.\] For every deterministic \(M\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}\) and every \(t>0\), \[\mathbb P(\|Y_z-M\|_F\le t\sqrt r) \le (C_\theta t)^{r^2}.\] The same bound holds conditionally when \(M\) and \(z\) are measurable with respect to a sigma-field independent of all \(g_i,k_i\), provided the two displayed conditions on \(z\) hold almost surely.

Proof

Condition on \(g_1,\ldots,g_N\) and write \[W_z=\sum_{i=1}^Nz_i^2g_ig_i^T.\] The columns of \(Y_z\) are conditionally independent Gaussian vectors with covariance \(W_z/r\). On the event that \(W_z\) is positive definite, their joint density is bounded by \[(2\pi)^{-r^2/2}\det(W_z/r)^{-r/2}.\] The moment estimate in Proposition 5.3 implies that \(W_z\) is positive definite almost surely: positive probability of \(\det W_z=0\) would make \(\mathbb E\det(W_z)^{-r/2}\) infinite.

The Euclidean volume of a ball of radius \(R\) in dimension \(m\) is at most \[\left(\frac{C R}{\sqrt m}\right)^m.\] Apply this with \(m=r^2\) and \(R=t\sqrt r\). The conditional density bound and the volume estimate give, uniformly in \(M\), \[\mathbb P(\|Y_z-M\|_F\le t\sqrt r\mid g_1,\ldots,g_N) \le(Ct)^{r^2}\det(W_z)^{-r/2}.\] Taking expectation and applying Proposition 5.3 proves the claim. For the conditional statement, condition first on the sigma-field that determines \(M\) and \(z\) and repeat the same argument pointwise.

QED.

5.3 Nuclear-ball covering and the block estimate

Let \(B_F\) denote the unit ball in Frobenius norm. For a bounded set \(T\), let \(N(T,\varepsilon B_F)\) be the least number of translates of \(\varepsilon B_F\) required to cover \(T\).

Lemma 5.6 (Nuclear-ball entropy).

Let \[K_{r,R}=\{A\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}:\|A\|_*\le R\}.\] There is a numerical constant \(C\) such that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[\log N(K_{r,R},\varepsilon B_F) \le C\frac{R^2r}{\varepsilon^2}.\] In particular, for \(R=\nu r\) and \(\varepsilon=t\sqrt r\), \[\log N(K_{r,\nu r},t\sqrt r B_F) \le C\frac{\nu^2}{t^2}r^2.\]

Proof

Let \(G\) be an \(r\) by \(r\) matrix with independent \(N(0,1)\) entries. Nuclear and operator norm duality gives \[\mathbb E\sup_{A\in K_{r,R}}\langle G,A\rangle =R\mathbb E\|G\|_{op}.\] The Gaussian comparison inequality applied to the process \(y^TGx\), with \(x,y\) on the Euclidean unit sphere, gives \[\mathbb E\|G\|_{op}\le2\sqrt r.\] Thus the Gaussian width of \(K_{r,R}\) is at most \(2R\sqrt r\).

Choose a maximal \(\varepsilon\)-separated subset of \(K_{r,R}\) in Frobenius norm. It is an \(\varepsilon\)-net. Sudakov’s minoration in the finite-set form [5, Section 2.1] says that an \(\varepsilon\)-separated set of cardinality \(M\) in the canonical metric of a Gaussian process has expected supremum at least \(c\varepsilon\sqrt{\log M}\). Applied here, it gives \[\varepsilon\sqrt{\log N(K_{r,R},\varepsilon B_F)} \le C\mathbb E\sup_{A\in K_{r,R}}\langle G,A\rangle.\] Squaring and using the width bound proves \[\log N(K_{r,R},\varepsilon B_F) \le C\frac{R^2r}{\varepsilon^2}.\] The stated specialization follows by substitution.

QED.

Theorem 5.7 (Arbitrary-center nuclear small ball).

Fix \(0<\theta<1/\sqrt2\). There are constants \(c_\theta,\nu_\theta>0\) such that the following holds under the hypotheses of Proposition 5.4. For every deterministic \(M\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}\), \[\mathbb P(\|Y_z-M\|_*\le\nu_\theta r) \le e^{-c_\theta r^2}.\] The same conclusion holds conditionally when \(M\) and \(z\) are measurable with respect to a sigma-field independent of the driver vectors and the stated conditions on \(z\) hold almost surely.

Proof

Let \(C_\theta\) be the constant in Lemma 5.4. Choose \(t_\theta>0\) so small that \[\log(C_\theta t_\theta)\le-2.\] By Lemma 5.6, the nuclear ball \(K_{r,\nu r}\) has a Frobenius \(t_\theta\sqrt r\)-net of cardinality at most \[\exp\left(C\frac{\nu^2}{t_\theta^2}r^2\right).\] If \(\|Y_z-M\|_*\le\nu r\), then for one center \(A\) in this net, \[\|Y_z-(M+A)\|_F\le t_\theta\sqrt r.\] The union bound and Proposition 5.4 therefore give \[\mathbb P(\|Y_z-M\|_*\le\nu r) \le \exp\left[ \left(C\frac{\nu^2}{t_\theta^2} +\log(C_\theta t_\theta)\right)r^2 \right].\] Choose \(\nu=\nu_\theta>0\) so small that \[C\frac{\nu_\theta^2}{t_\theta^2}\le1.\] The exponent is then at most \(-r^2\), and the theorem follows after changing the constant. The conditional statement follows because both the Frobenius small-ball estimate and the covering number are uniform in the center.

QED.

Lemma 7.3 (Rectangular pinching).

Fix \(q>1\). For \(u=0\) set \(V=W=0\). For \(u\ne0\) put \(\lambda_{\max}=\|u\|_\infty\) and define \[B_t=\{i:2^{-t-1}\lambda_{\max}<|u_i| \le2^{-t}\lambda_{\max}\},\quad t\ge0.\]

Empty levels are ignored. Let \(t_1\) be the first nonempty level. Given \(t_r\), let \(t_{r+1}\) be the first later nonempty level satisfying \[|B_{t_{r+1}}|\ge q|B_{t_r}|,\] if it exists; otherwise the construction stops and the last group contains all levels after its selected level. Put \[S_r=|B_{t_r}|,\quad \lambda_r=2^{-t_r-1}\lambda_{\max},\]

and \(G_r=\{t:t_r\le t<t_{r+1}\}\) with \(t_{R+1}=\infty\) for the last record \(R\). Every nonempty nonselected level \(t\in G_r\setminus\{t_r\}\) satisfies \(|B_t|<qS_r\).

Fix an integer \(T\ge1\). In group \(r\), retain only levels whose offset \(j=t-t_r\) lies in \(\{0,\dots,T-1\}\). Let \(V_r\) be the restriction of \(u\) to \(\bigcup_{j=0}^{T-1}B_{t_r+j}\cap G_r\), let \(W_r\) be the restriction to \(\bigcup_{j\ge T}B_{t_r+j}\cap G_r\), and put \[V=\sum_rV_r,\quad W=u-V=\sum_rW_r,\]

\[k_r=|\operatorname{supp}V_r|,\quad m_r=\|V_r\|_1,\quad Q_r=\|V_r\|_2^2,\quad R_r=\frac{m_r^2}{Q_r}.\]

Supports of distinct \(V_r\) are disjoint and \(m_r>0\), \(Q_r>0\) because \(B_{t_r}\subset\operatorname{supp}V_r\).

Every nonselected level in group \(r\) has fewer than \(qS_r\) entries. The base level has entries in

\((\lambda_r,2\lambda_r]\), while a level at offset \(j\) has entries at most \(2\lambda_r2^{-j}\). Therefore \[S_r\le k_r\le TqS_r,\]

\[\lambda_rS_r\le m_r\le2\lambda_rS_r +2q\lambda_rS_r\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j} \le4q\lambda_rS_r,\]

and \[\lambda_r^2S_r\le Q_r\le4\lambda_r^2S_r +4q\lambda_r^2S_r\sum_{j\ge1}4^{-j} \le\frac{16q}{3}\lambda_r^2S_r.\]

Consequently, \[\frac3{16q}S_r\le R_r\le16q^2S_r.\]

Also, \[\|V_r\|_\infty\le2\lambda_r\le8q\frac{Q_r}{m_r} =8q\frac{\|V_r\|_2}{\sqrt{R_r}}.\]

The omitted mass in group \(r\) is bounded by \[\|W_r\|_1\le2q\lambda_rS_r\sum_{j\ge T}2^{-j} =4q2^{-T}\lambda_rS_r\le4q2^{-T}m_r.\]

Summing gives \[\|W\|_1\le\epsilon_T\|u\|_1,\quad \epsilon_T=4q2^{-T}.\] The selected support sizes grow as \(S_{r+1}\ge qS_r\). Hence \[\sum_{l<r}S_l\le\frac{S_r}{q-1}\]

and \[\sum_{l<r}k_l\le\frac{Tq}{q-1}S_r.\]

These are deterministic statements. Their constants depend only on \(q,T\) and are independent of coefficient signs, the number of empty levels, and the dynamic range.

Remark 7.6 (A small example).

Take \(q=2\) and suppose the nonempty dyadic levels have support sizes \(1,1,3,2,10\) at successive levels. The selected support records have sizes \(1,3,10\): the second level is not selected because \(1<2\cdot1\), the third is selected because \(3\ge2\cdot1\), the fourth is not selected because \(2<2\cdot3\), and the fifth is selected because \(10\ge2\cdot3\). Thus the first two dyadic levels form the first group, the next two form the second, and the final level begins the third. The actual coefficient values and their signs play no role in this selection.

7.5 One regular dyadic group after removing earlier sample spans

Lemma 7.7.

Fix \(c_{\rm rank},C_{\rm reg}>0\). There are constants \(C_{\rm low},c_{\rm up},c_{\rm proj}>0\), depending only on \(c_{\rm rank},C_{\rm reg}\), for which the following holds. Let \(H,J\subset[n]\) be disjoint deterministic supports. Let \(P_H,Q_H\) project away from the left and right sample spans indexed by \(H\). Suppose their residual ranks are at least \(c_{\rm rank}d\).

Let \(u\) be deterministic and supported on \(J\). Put \[m=\|u\|_1, \sigma=\|u\|_2, R=\frac{m^2}{\sigma^2}.\] Assume \[\|u\|_\infty\le C_{\rm reg}\frac\sigma{\sqrt R}, C_{\rm low}\log d\le R\le c_{\rm up}d.\] Then, almost surely with respect to \(\sigma(a_i,b_i:i\in H)\), \[\mathbb P\left(\|P_H\mathcal{B}uQ_H\|_*<c_{\rm proj} \sqrt d m\middle|\sigma(a_i,b_i:i\in H)\right)\le e^{-c_{\rm proj}dR}.\]

Proof

Choose measurable orthonormal bases of the residual spaces. Conditional on the head sigma-field, the projected samples indexed by \(J\) have independent standard Gaussian coordinates in those bases.

Fix \(\theta_0=1/2\). Choose \(\gamma>0\) such that \[C_{\rm reg}\sqrt\gamma\le\theta_0<1/\sqrt2,\] and put \(r=\lfloor\gamma R\rfloor\). Increasing \(C_{\rm low}\) if needed gives \(r\ge c\log d\), while decreasing \(c_{\rm up}\) makes \(r\) smaller than a fixed fraction of each residual rank.

Partition each residual coordinate space into \[J_0=\left\lfloor\frac{\min\{\operatorname{rank} P_H,\operatorname{rank}Q_H\}}r\right\rfloor\] blocks of side \(r\), and pair blocks with the same index. Then \(J_0\ge c d/r\).

For a paired block, write \[M_j=\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{i\in J}u_i g_{ij}k_{ij}^T = \frac{\sigma\sqrt r}{\sqrt d}Y_j,\] where \[Y_j=\frac1{\sqrt r}\sum_{i\in J}\frac{u_i}{\sigma} g_{ij}k_{ij}^T.\] The coefficient cap is \[\sqrt r\max_i\frac{|u_i|}{\sigma} \le C_{\rm reg}\sqrt{\frac rR} \le\theta_0.\] The arbitrary-center estimate Lemma 7.2, with \(\theta=\theta_0\) and center zero, gives \[\mathbb P\left(\|M_j\|_*<\nu_{\rm sb}\sigma \frac{r^{3/2}}{\sqrt d}\middle|\sigma(a_i,b_i:i\in H)\right)\le p_r,\] where \(p_r=e^{-c_{\rm sb}r^2}\). The events for different paired blocks are conditionally independent because they use disjoint coordinates of every current \(a_i\) and \(b_i\).

Let \(N_{bad}\) be the number of bad blocks. For all large \(d\), \[\mathbb P(N_{bad}\ge J_0/2\mid\sigma(a_i,b_i:i\in H)) \le \sum_{s\ge J_0/2}\binom{J_0}{s}p_r^s \le 2^{J_0}p_r^{J_0/2}.\] Since \(p_r=e^{-c_{\rm sb}r^2}\) and \(J_0r\ge cd\), the last expression is at most \(e^{-cJ_0r^2}\le e^{-cdr}\).

On the complementary event, Lemma 7.3 gives \[\|P_H\mathcal{B}uQ_H\|_* \ge \sum_{j=1}^{J_0}\|M_j\|_* \ge \frac{J_0}{2} \nu_{\rm sb}\sigma\frac{r^{3/2}}{\sqrt d} \ge c\sigma\sqrt{dr}.\] Since \(r\asymp R\) and \(\sigma\sqrt R=m\), the last quantity is at least \(c\sqrt d m\). Also \(dr\asymp dR\). This proves the lemma.

QED.

7.6 The small-support prefix and the point where span removal stops

Let \(j_{\rm pre}\) be the largest group index for which \[k_{\rm pre}:=\sum_{r\le j_{\rm pre}}k_{r}\le s_{sp}.\] If there is no such selected group, put \(j_{\rm pre}=0\), \(k_{\rm pre}=0\), and \(V_{0}=0\). Otherwise, put \(V_{0}=\sum_{r\le j_{\rm pre}}V_{r}\). On \(E_{sp}\), \[\|\mathcal{B}V_{0}\|_{*}\ge c_{s}^{2}\sqrt{d}\|V_{0}\|_{1}.\] If selected group \(j_{\rm pre}+1\) exists, then \[S_{j_{\rm pre}+1}\ge c_{q,T}\frac{d}{\log d}.\] Indeed, first suppose \(k_{\rm pre}>s_{sp}/2\). The cumulative support estimate gives \(k_{\rm pre}\le C_{q,T}S_{j_{\rm pre}}\), so geometric growth gives \(S_{j_{\rm pre}+1}\ge qS_{j_{\rm pre}}\ge c_{q,T}s_{sp}\). If \(k_{\rm pre}\le s_{sp}/2\), maximality gives \(k_{j_{\rm pre}+1}>s_{sp}/2\), while \(k_{j_{\rm pre}+1}\le TqS_{j_{\rm pre}+1}\). The same conclusion follows. This also covers \(j_{\rm pre}=0\).

The deterministic dyadic-group estimates imply \[\|V_{r}\|_{\infty}\le8q\frac{\|V_{r}\|_{2}} {\sqrt{R_{r}}}.\] Apply Lemma 7.7 with \(c_{\rm rank}=1/4\) and \(C_{\rm reg}=8q\), and let \(C_{\rm low},c_{\rm up}\) be the resulting effective-support thresholds. Fix \[0<\rho<\min\left\{\frac18, \frac{c_{\rm up}}{32q^{2}}\right\}.\] For all sufficiently large \(d\), this gives \[h-\rho d\ge\frac{d}{4}.\] Starting after the small-support prefix, retain selected groups only while \[\sum_{l\le r}k_{l}\le\rho d.\] Before each retained selected group, both residual ranks are at least \(h-\rho d\). Moreover, \(S_{r}\le k_{r}\le\rho d\), so \[R_{r}\le16q^{2}S_{r}\le16q^{2}\rho d<c_{\rm up}d.\] Every retained selected group outside the small-support prefix also has \[R_{r}\asymp_{q}S_{r}\ge c_{q,T}\frac{d}{\log d} \ge C_{\rm low}\log d\] for all sufficiently large \(d\). Thus Lemma 7.7 applies with the displayed fixed parameters.

If \(r_{*}\) is the first selected group not treated by span removal, then \[S_{r_{*}}\ge c_{q,T,\rho}d.\] Indeed, the support through \(r_{*}\) exceeds \(\rho d\), while \[\sum_{l\le r_{*}}k_{l}\le Tq\sum_{l\le r_{*}}S_{l}\le \frac{Tq^{2}}{q-1}S_{r_{*}}.\] Since \(S_{r}\le|\operatorname{supp}u|\le Kd\) and the selected support sizes grow by \(q\), the number of remaining groups satisfies \[J_{\rm late}\le1+\log_{q} \left(\frac{C_{q}KT}{\rho}\right).\]

7.7 Combining nuclear-norm witnesses on orthogonal subspaces

For each retained group we construct a matrix of operator norm at most one that attains the nuclear norm of that group’s projected contribution. The left and right support spaces for different groups are orthogonal. The witnesses can therefore be added without increasing the operator norm above one.

For a matrix \(M\), define \[\operatorname{pol}(M)=M(M^TM)^{\dagger/2},\] where \(A^{\dagger/2}\) acts by \(\lambda^{-1/2}\) on every positive eigenspace of \(A\) and by zero on its kernel. Thus \(\|\operatorname{pol}(M)\|_{op}\le1\) and \(\langle\operatorname{pol}(M),M\rangle=\|M\|_*\). Let \(L_0,R_0\) project onto the left and right sample spans of the sparse prefix, put \(M_0=\mathcal{B}V_0\), and define \(Z_0=\operatorname{pol}(M_0)\). Then \(Z_0=L_0Z_0R_0\); set \(Z_0=0\) when \(M_0=0\).

For a selected group \(r\) treated by span removal, let \(P_{r-1},Q_{r-1}\) project away from every retained sample span preceding \(r\), and let \(P_r,Q_r\) also remove the current sample spans. Define \[D_r=P_{r-1}-P_r, E_r=Q_{r-1}-Q_r.\] Nestedness makes \(D_r,E_r\) orthogonal projections, and different increments are pairwise orthogonal. Moreover, \[P_{r-1}\mathcal{B}V_rQ_{r-1} = D_rP_{r-1}\mathcal{B}V_rQ_{r-1}E_r.\] Indeed, nestedness gives \(P_rP_{r-1}=P_r\). For every current index \(i\), one also has \(P_ra_i=0\). Therefore \[D_rP_{r-1}a_i=(P_{r-1}-P_r)P_{r-1}a_i=P_{r-1}a_i.\] The same calculation on the right gives \(E_rQ_{r-1}b_i=Q_{r-1}b_i\).

Put \(M_r=P_{r-1}\mathcal{B}V_rQ_{r-1}\) and \(Z_r=\operatorname{pol}(M_r)\). Then \[Z_r=D_rZ_rE_r, \|Z_r\|_{op}\le1.\] The prefix support projections and all increments are mutually orthogonal. Hence, for \[Z=Z_0+\sum_{r\text{ captured}}Z_r,\] the same calculation as in Lemma 7.3 gives \(Z^TZ\preceq I\) and therefore \[\|Z\|_{op}\le1.\] If \(k<r\), every left sample in \(V_k\) lies in the span removed before increment \(D_r\). Thus \[\langle Z_r,\mathcal{B}V_k\rangle=0.\] All earlier cross terms vanish pathwise. Orthogonal projection and the canonical partial polar map can be chosen as Borel functions of the samples.

7.8 Controlling cross terms from later coefficient groups

The witness for an earlier group also pairs with later groups. These terms have conditional mean zero but are not bounded almost surely. We use their exact conditional moment generating function. The quantity \(B_{mart}\) below is the reciprocal radius on which that moment generating function is finite; it is not an almost-sure bound on an increment.

Fix deterministic \(u\) and its dyadic decomposition. Let \(\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\) contain all samples in the small-support prefix and in retained groups preceding group \(k\). Let \[Z_{<k}=Z_0+\sum_{\substack{r<k\\r\text{ captured}}}Z_r\] and define \[X_k=\langle Z_{<k},\mathcal{B}V_k\rangle.\] Then \(Z_{<k}\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\)-measurable and the current cluster samples are independent of \(\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\).

For independent \(a,b\sim N(0,I_h)\) and fixed \(Z\), \[\mathbb Ee^{\xi a^TZb}=\det(I-\xi^2ZZ^T)^{-1/2}\] when \(|\xi|\|Z\|_{op}<1\). To verify it, condition on \(a\), integrate in \(b\), and then use the Gaussian quadratic-form formula.

Independence across coefficient indices gives \[\mathbb E(e^{tX_k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}) = \prod_{i\in \operatorname{supp}V_k} \det\left(I-\frac{t^2(V_k)_i^2} {d}Z_{<k}Z_{<k}^T\right)^{-1/2}.\] If \[|t|\frac{\|V_k\|_\infty\|Z_{<k}\|_{op}}{\sqrt d}\le \frac1{\sqrt2},\] then every eigenvalue in the logarithm is at most \(1/2\). Since \(-\log(1-x)\le2x\) on \([0,1/2]\), \[\mathbb E(e^{tX_k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}) \le \exp\left(\frac{t^2Q_k\|Z_{<k}\|_F^2}{d}\right).\] Thus the conditional MGF estimate holds for both signs of \(t\).

Every polar witness has squared Frobenius norm at most its supporting rank. Orthogonality and the cumulative support bound give \[\|Z_{<k}\|_F^2 \le \sum_{l<k}k_l \le C_{q,T}S_k.\] The deterministic dyadic-group estimates give \[Q_k\le C_q\frac{m_k^2}{S_k}\] and \[\|V_k\|_\infty\le C_q\frac{m_k}{S_k}.\] Put \(M=\sum_km_k=\|V\|_1\). Let \[v_k=\frac{CQ_k\|Z_{<k}\|_F^2}{d}.\] The predictable variance sum has the deterministic upper bound \[\sum_kv_k \le \frac{C_{q,T}}d\sum_km_k^2 \le \frac{C_{q,T}}dM^2=:V_{mart}.\] Every cluster after the sparse prefix has \(S_k\ge c_{q,T}d/\log d\). Therefore the reciprocal MGF radius is \[B_{mart}:=\max_k\frac{\|V_k\|_\infty}{\sqrt d} \le C_{q,T}\frac{M\log d}{d^{3/2}}.\] After the homogeneous normalization \(M=1\), these are exactly \(V_{mart}\le C_{q,T}/d\) and \(B_{mart}\le C_{q,T}\log d/d^{3/2}\). The second quantity is an MGF-radius parameter. It is not an almost-sure increment bound.

For \(0\le\lambda\le c/B_{mart}\), iteration of the conditional MGF bound first gives \[\mathbb E\exp\left(-\lambda\sum_kX_k-\lambda^2\sum_kv_k\right) \le1.\] Since \(\sum_kv_k\le V_{mart}\) pathwise, it follows that \[\mathbb E\exp\left(-\lambda\sum_kX_k\right)\le e^{\lambda^2V_{mart}}.\] Markov’s inequality gives \[\mathbb P\left(\sum_kX_k\le-x\right) \le \inf_{0\le\lambda\le c/B_{mart}} \exp(-\lambda x+\lambda^2V_{mart}).\] Choose \[\lambda=\min\left\{\frac{x}{2V_{mart}},\frac{c} {2B_{mart}}\right\}.\] Then \[\mathbb P\left(\sum_kX_k\le-x\right) \le \exp\left[-c\min\left\{\frac{x^2}{V_{mart}}, \frac{x}{B_{mart}}\right\}\right].\] At \(x=a_{\rm mart}M\sqrt d\), where \(a_{\rm mart}>0\) will be chosen below, \[\frac{x^2}{V_{mart}}\ge c_{q,T}d^2, \frac{x}{B_{mart}}\ge c_{q,T}\frac{d^2}{\log d}.\] Thus \[\mathbb P\left(\sum_kX_k<-a_{\rm mart}M\sqrt d\right) \le \exp\left(-c_{q,T}\frac{d^2}{\log d}\right).\]

7.9 When one remaining dyadic block has macroscopic support

If the groups treated above carry less than half of the coefficient mass, then one of the remaining dyadic blocks has support comparable to \(d\). We condition on every sample outside that block. The other contributions then form an arbitrary measurable matrix center, while the selected block remains independent and can be handled by the arbitrary-center small-ball estimate.

Lemma 7.8.

Fix \(c_0,K>0\). Let \(S\subset[n]\), \(s=|S|\), suppose \[c_0d\le s\le Kd,\] and suppose \[\lambda\le|u_i|\le2\lambda\] for \(i\in S\). Let \(C\) be measurable with respect to \[\mathcal{G}_S=\sigma(a_i,b_i:i\notin S).\] Then there is a numerical constant \(c_{\rm ld}>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(d\) (with the starting dimension allowed to depend on \(c_0,K\)), almost surely with respect to \(\mathcal{G}_S\), \[\mathbb P\left(\|C+\mathcal{B}u_S\|_*<c_{\rm ld}\sqrt d \|u_S\|_1\sqrt{\frac{\min\{s,d\}}s}\middle| \mathcal{G}_S\right) \le e^{-c_{\rm ld}d\min\{s,d\}}.\]

Proof

Put \(\sigma=\|u_S\|_2\) and choose \[r=\lfloor\gamma\min\{s,d\}\rfloor\] with a fixed numerical \(\gamma>0\) small. Since \(s\ge c_0d\), after increasing the starting dimension if necessary, \[r\ge\frac\gamma2\min\{s,d\}.\] Also, since \(\sigma\ge\lambda\sqrt s\), \[\sqrt r\frac{\|u_S\|_\infty}{\sigma} \le 2\sqrt\gamma<1/\sqrt2.\] Partition both coordinate spaces into \(J_0\ge cd/r\) paired \(r\) by \(r\) blocks. Conditional on \(\mathcal{G}_S\), every block of \(C\) is fixed and the selected coefficient block uses independent Gaussian coordinates in distinct paired coordinate blocks. For each paired block, Lemma 7.2 shows that the conditional probability that its nuclear norm is smaller than \[c\sigma\frac{r^{3/2}}{\sqrt d}\] is at most \(e^{-cr^2}\). Let \(N_{good}\) denote the number of paired blocks whose nuclear norm is at least this threshold. The binomial calculation in Lemma 7.7 gives \[\mathbb P(N_{good}<J_0/2\mid\mathcal{G}_S)\le e^{-cJ_0r^2}\le e^{-cdr}.\] On the complementary event, the pinching estimate Lemma 7.3 gives \[\|C+\mathcal{B}u_S\|_* \ge c\frac dr\sigma \frac{r^{3/2}}{\sqrt d} = c\sigma\sqrt{dr}.\] Flatness gives \(\sigma\ge\|u_S\|_1/(2\sqrt s)\). Since \(r\asymp\min\{s,d\}\), the last display proves the asserted scale and exponent.

QED.

Suppose now that less than half of \(M=\|V\|_1\) lies in the small-support prefix and the groups treated by span removal. The remaining groups carry more than \(M/2\). There are at most \(J_{late}\) of them, so one remaining group has mass at least \(M/(2J_{late})\). Its base dyadic bin has mass at least a \(1/(4q)\) fraction of its group mass, because the group upper bound is \(4q\lambda_rS_r\).

Call this base bin \(S\). It is deterministic once the coefficient vector is fixed. It satisfies \[c_{q,T,\rho}d\le|S|\le Kd.\] Reveal \(\mathcal{G}_S\). The center \[C=\mathcal{B}(V-u_S)\] is \(\mathcal{G}_S\)-measurable, while the samples in the selected block remain independent of \(\mathcal{G}_S\). The macroscopic-block estimate Lemma 7.8, applied with \(c_0=c_{q,T,\rho}\) and the preceding fixed \(K\), therefore gives, almost surely with respect to \(\mathcal{G}_S\), \[\mathbb P\left(\|\mathcal{B}V\|_*<\frac{c_{\rm ld}\sqrt d} {8q\sqrt{K_+}J_{late}}M\middle|\mathcal{G}_S\right) \le e^{-c_{q,T,\rho,K}d^2}.\]

7.10 Combining the two alternatives for one fixed coefficient vector

By homogeneity, normalize \(\|u\|_1=1\). Let \(T\) be specified below and put \(M=\|V\|_1\ge1-\epsilon_T\).

Let \(M_{cap}\) be the mass in the small-support prefix and the groups treated by span removal. If \(M_{cap}\ge M/2\), the sparse and projected lower bounds give a diagonal polar pairing at least \[c\sqrt dM_{cap}\ge c\sqrt dM.\] By Section 7.7, the aggregate witness has operator norm at most one and all backward terms vanish. The supermartingale estimate in Section 7.8 gives \[\mathbb P\left(\sum_kX_k<-cM\sqrt d\right)\le \exp\left(-c_{q,T}\frac{d^2}{\log d}\right).\] Choose \(a_{\rm mart}\) to be half the diagonal constant. Then \[\|\mathcal{B}V\|_*\ge c\sqrt dM.\] For each projected group \(r\), let \(F_r\) be the event that the lower bound from Lemma 7.7 fails. Every such \(R_r\) outside the small-support prefix is at least \(c_{q,T}d/\log d\), and there are at most \(1+\log_q(Kd)\) groups. The tower property and a union bound give \[\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_rF_r\right)\le C\log d\exp\left(-c_{q,T}\frac{d^2}{\log d}\right).\] If \(M_{cap}<M/2\), let \(F_{late}\) be the event that the following lower bound fails: \[\|\mathcal{B}V\|_* \ge \frac{c} {\sqrt{K_+}[1+\log_q(C_qK_+T/\rho)]}\sqrt dM.\] The conditional estimate in Section 7.9, followed by the tower property, gives \[\mathbb P(F_{late})\le e^{-c_{q,T,\rho,K}d^2}.\] Combining the branches, for every fixed deterministic \(u\), \[\mathbb P\left(\{\|\mathcal{B}V\|_*<c_{ret}(K,T)\sqrt d \|V\|_1\}\cap E_{sp}\right) \le C\log d\exp\left(-c_{q,T,K}\frac{d^2}{\log d}\right),\] where \[c_{ret}(K,T) \ge \frac{c} {\sqrt{K_+}[1+\log_q(C_qK_+T/\rho)]}.\] On \(E_{atom}\), \[\|\mathcal{B}W\|_*\le C_A\epsilon_T\sqrt d\|u\|_1.\] Therefore \[\|\mathcal{B}u\|_* \ge \left[c_{ret}(K,T)(1- \epsilon_T)-C_A\epsilon_T\right]\sqrt d\|u\|_1.\] Now fix \(q=2\) and \(\rho\) as above. Set \[T=\left\lceil T_0+2\log_2(eK_+)\right\rceil.\] The lower constants in the small-support-prefix and projected-group arguments depend on \(q\) and \(\rho\), but not on \(T\), and the macroscopic-block scale constant \(c_{\rm ld}\) is numerical. The truncation depth affects the cumulative support factor, the exceptional exponent, the starting dimension, and the number of remaining groups. Thus the retained lower-bound scale loses only the factor \(1+\log K_+\) displayed below.

The ratio \[\frac{(1+\log(K_+T))}{K_+^{3/2}}\] is bounded on \(K_+\ge1\). Hence \(T_0\), depending only on the atom constant, can be chosen so that \[C_A\epsilon_T\le\frac14c_{ret}(K,T)\] for every fixed \(K\). With \(T_0\) increased if needed, \(\epsilon_T\le1/2\). Define \(c_{\rm ms}(A)>0\) to be a sufficiently small constant depending only on the single-summand bound for aspect parameter \(A\). It follows that \[\|\mathcal{B}u\|_* \ge \frac{c_{\rm ms}(A)} {\sqrt{K_+}(1+\log K_+)}\sqrt d\|u\|_1.\] If \(F_u\) denotes the event that this inequality fails, then, for every fixed deterministic \(u\), \[\mathbb P(F_u\cap E_{sp}\cap E_{atom})\le C\log d\exp\left(-c_{q,T,K}\frac{d^2}{\log d}\right).\]

7.11 Uniformity over every support and coefficient vector

Normalize \(\|u\|_1=1\). Denote the fixed-vector constant obtained in Section 7.10 by \[c^{fix}_{K,A}=\frac{c_{\rm ms}(A)} {\sqrt{K_+}(1+\log K_+)}.\] For each support \(S\) with \(|S|\le Kd\), take a deterministic \(\delta\)-net of its \(\ell_1\) unit sphere in \(\ell_1\) distance, with \[\delta=\frac{c^{fix}_{K,A}}{4C_A}.\] A volumetric packing argument gives at most \[(1+4/\delta)^{|S|}\] points. Thus \[\log N_{coef}\le Kd\log(1+4/\delta)=O_{A,K}(d).\] If \(Kd\le n/2\), then \[\log N_{supp} \le Kd\log\left(\frac{en}{Kd}\right) +O_K(d)=O_{A,K}(d\log d).\] If \(Kd>n/2\), then either \(n=O_K(d)\) and \(2^n=e^{O_K(d)}\), or one may use the same upper bound after replacing \(Kd\) by \(\min\{Kd,n\}\). In every case, \[\log N_{supp}=O_{A,K}(d\log d).\] Each deterministic net point determines its dyadic decomposition. Absolute dyadic locations, gaps between selected support sizes, group partitions, and stopping indices are deterministic functions of that point. Projections and nuclear-norm witnesses are Borel functions of the revealed Gaussian samples and are handled by conditional probability. Thus the support and coefficient nets account for all entropy terms.

The union-bound exponent is \[-c'_{K,A}\frac{d^2}{\log d}+C_{A,K}d\log d \longrightarrow-\infty.\] Indeed, if \(E_{net}\) denotes validity at every net point, then \[\mathbb P(E_{net}^c\cap E_{sp}\cap E_{atom}) \le \exp\left[-c'_{K,A}\frac{d^2}{\log d}+C_{A,K}d\log d\right].\] If \(u_0\) is a net point with \(\|u-u_0\|_1\le\delta\), then on \(E_{atom}\), \[\|\mathcal{B}(u-u_0)\|_* \le C_A\sqrt d\delta \le \frac14c^{fix}_{K,A}\sqrt d.\] Both \(u\) and \(u_0\) have \(\ell_1\)-norm one, so the net lower bound extends with a fixed factor loss. Recall that the Gaussian width of \(\mathcal P_p\) is defined as \[w(\mathcal P_p) = \mathbb E\sup_{D \in \mathcal P_p} \langle H, D \rangle\] where \(H\) is a symmetric isonormal Gaussian operator. Now, \[H = H_{+} - H_{-},\; H_{\pm} \succeq 0,\; H_{+} H_{-} = 0,\; \|H\|_{F}^{2} = \|H_{+}\|_{F}^{2} + \|H_{-}\|_{F}^{2}.\] Therefore, \[\sup_{D \in \mathcal P_p} \langle H, D \rangle \le \|H_+\|_F\] and \[\mathbb E\|H\|_{F}^{2} = \mathbb E\|H_{+}\|_{F}^{2} + \mathbb E\|H_{-}\|_{F}^{2}.\]

Now, since the law of \(H\) is invariant under \(H \mapsto -H\), \[\mathbb E\|H_+\|_F^2=\frac12\mathbb E\|H\|_F^2=\frac{p(p+1)}4.\]

By Jensen’s inequality, the Gaussian width satisfies \[w(\mathcal P_p) \le \sqrt{\frac{p (p + 1)}{4}}.\]

QED.

Lemma 13.4.

Let \(G_1, \dots, G_n\) be mutually independent isonormal Gaussian operators on \(\operatorname{Sym}_p\). Let \(L \subseteq \mathbb R^n\) be a deterministic linear subspace of dimension \(m_L\). Fix a deterministic retained row set \(J \subseteq [n]\) with \(|J| = m\). Let \(L_J = \{v_J : v \in L\}\). If \(U_J\) is deterministic and has orthonormal rows spanning \(L_J^\perp\), then \(U_J G_J\) is an \(r_J\)-row isonormal Gaussian operator, where \[r_J = m - \dim L_J \ge m - m_L.\]

Proof

Since \(L\) is deterministic, so is \(L_J\), and \(\dim L_J \le \dim L = m_L\) by the rank-nullity theorem. Hence \[r_J = m - \dim L_J \ge m - m_L.\] If \(r_J = 0\) there is nothing else to prove, so assume \(r_J \geq 1\).

Recall that \(\{Q_a\}_a\) is an orthonormal basis of \(\operatorname{Sym}_p\) and write \(\xi_{ia} = \langle G_i, Q_a\rangle = G_i(Q_a)\). Since \(G_1, \dots, G_n\) are independent isonormal, then \(\xi_{ia}\) are independent \(N(0, 1)\) in \(a\), with \[\mathbb E[\xi_{ia} \xi_{jb}] = \delta_{ij} \delta_{ab}.\]

Put \(G_J = (G_j)_{j \in J}\). Then \[(U_J G_J)_r = \sum_{j \in J} U_{rj} G_j, \qquad (U_J \xi)_{ra} = \sum_{j \in J} U_{rj} \xi_{ja},\] and \[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E[(U_J \xi)_{ra} (U_J \xi)_{sb}] &=\sum_{j, k} U_{rj} U_{sk} \mathbb E[\xi_{ja} \xi_{kb}] \\ &=\sum_j U_{rj} U_{sj} \delta_{ab} = (U_J U_J^\top)_{rs} \delta_{ab} = \delta_{rs}\delta_{ab}, \end{aligned}\] since rows are orthonormal.

Jointly Gaussian with identity covariance implies that \((U_J \xi)_{ra}\) are independent \(N(0,1)\) in \(r\), \(a\), and the claim follows.

QED.

13.3 Gaussian escape inequality

For completeness, the exact Gaussian escape inequality used here follows from the Gaussian min-max comparison. Recall that \[\mathcal P_p = \{D \in \operatorname{Sym}_p : D \succeq 0 \text{~and~} \|D\|_F = 1\}.\]

Lemma 13.5.

Let \(G_{1}, \dots, G_{r}\) be independent isonormal Gaussians on \(\operatorname{Sym}_p\), and let \(A : \operatorname{Sym}_p \to \mathbb R^{r}\) be a random linear operator defined by \[(A D)_{j} = \langle G_{j}, D \rangle \qquad \text{for $j = 1, \ldots, r$}.\] Let \(g_{r} \sim N(0, I_{r})\) be independent of \(A\).

Then for \(t > 0\) \[\mathbb P\Bigl(\inf_{D \in \mathcal P_{p}} \|A D\|_{2} < \mathbb E\|g_{r}\|_{2} - w(\mathcal P_{p}) - t\Bigr) \le e^{-t^{2} / 2}.\]

Proof

For any \(O : \operatorname{Sym}_p \to \mathbb R^{r}\) define \[f(O) = \inf_{D \in \mathcal P_{p}} \|O D\|_{2}.\] It is not hard to see that \[f(O) = \inf_{D \in \mathcal P_{p}} \sup_{y \in S^{r - 1}} \langle y, O D \rangle,\] where \[S^{r - 1} = \{y \in \mathbb R^{r} : \|y\|_{2} = 1\},\] so \(f\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz.

Let \(h\) be isonormal on \(\operatorname{Sym}_p\), independent of \(A\) and \(g_r\). On \(\mathcal P_{p}\times S^{r - 1}\) set \[X_{D,y} = \langle g_{r}, y \rangle + \langle h, D \rangle, \qquad Y_{D,y} = \langle y, A D \rangle = \sum_{j = 1}^r y_{j} \langle G_{j}, D \rangle.\] Both are centred Gaussian, continuous in \((D, y)\) for fixed \(A\), so finite Theorem A [6, Theorem A] extends to compact sets as in [6, Cor 1.2]. Theorem A states that if \[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E|X_{ij} - X_{ik}|^{2} &\le \mathbb E|Y_{ij} - Y_{ik}|^{2}, \\ \mathbb E|X_{ij} - X_{\ell k}|^{2} &\ge \mathbb E|Y_{ij} - Y_{\ell k}|^{2} \quad \text{for $i \ne \ell$}, \end{aligned}\] then \(\mathbb E\min_{i}\max_{j} X_{ij} \le \mathbb E\min_{i}\max_{j} Y_{ij}\). Equal variances are not required, only increments. In our case \(i = D\), \(j = y\).

Put \(a = \langle y, y' \rangle \in [-1,1]\), \(b = \langle D, D' \rangle_{F} \in [0,1]\) for \(D, D' \succeq 0\). For \(\|D\|_{F} = 1\), \(\|y\|_{2} = 1\), \[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E\langle G_{j}, D \rangle \langle G_{j}, D' \rangle &= b, \\ \mathbb E(y_{j} \langle G_{j}, D \rangle - y'_{j} \langle G_{j}, D' \rangle)^{2} &= y_{j}^{2} + y_{j}'^{2} - 2 b y_{j} y'_{j}. \end{aligned}\] Summing in \(j\), \[\begin{aligned} \mathbb E|Y_{D, y} - Y_{D', y'}|^{2} &= 2 (1 - a b), \\ \mathbb E|X_{D, y} - X_{D', y'}|^{2} &= \|y - y'\|_{2}^{2} + \|D - D'\|_{F}^{2} = 2 (1 - a) + 2 (1 - b). \end{aligned}\] Hence \[\mathbb E|X - X'|^{2} - \mathbb E|Y - Y'|^{2} = 2 (1 - a) (1 - b) \ge 0\] with equality if \(D' = D\) (\(b = 1\)) or \(y' = y\) (\(a = 1\)). Thus, conditions of Theorem A hold, and \[\mathbb E\min_{D} \max_{y} X_{D, y} \le \mathbb E\min_{D} \max_{y} Y_{D, y}.\]

Now \(\max_{y \in S^{r - 1}} \langle g_{r}, y \rangle = \|g_{r}\|_{2}\), \(\max_{y} \langle y, A D \rangle = \|A D\|_{2}\), so \[\max_{y}X_{D,y}= \|g_{r}\|_{2} + \langle h, D \rangle, \qquad \max_{y}Y_{D,y}= \|AD\|_{2},\] therefore \[\min_{D} \max_{y} X_{D,y} = \|g_{r}\|_{2} + \inf_{D}\langle h, D \rangle, \qquad \min_{D} \max_{y} Y_{D,y} = f(A).\] With \(w(\mathcal P_{p}) = \mathbb E\sup_{D}\langle h, D\rangle\) and \(-h\stackrel{d}=h\), \[\mathbb E\inf_{D}\langle h,D\rangle=-w(\mathcal P_{p})\] and \(g_{r} \perp h\), hence \[\mathbb Ef(A) = \mathbb E\min_{D} \max_{y} Y \ge \mathbb E\min_{D} \max_{y} X = \mathbb E\|g_{r}\|_{2} - w(\mathcal P_{p}).\]

Since \(f\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz, by Gaussian concentration [6, Theorem 3.2], \[\mathbb P\bigl(f(A) < \mathbb Ef(A) - t\bigr) \le e^{-t^{2}/2} \qquad \text{for $t > 0$}.\] Therefore, \[\mathbb P\Bigl(\inf_{D}\|A D\|_{2} < \mathbb E\|g_{r}\|_{2} - w(\mathcal P_{p}) - t\Bigr) \le e^{-t^{2}/2}.\]

QED.

13.4 A robust Gaussian loss margin

Lemma 13.6.

Let \(G_1, \dots, G_n\) be mutually independent isonormal Gaussian operators on \(\operatorname{Sym}_p\). Fix \(\epsilon > 0\) and \(0 < C_1 < \infty\). Let \(L \subseteq \mathbb R^n\) be a deterministic linear subspace of dimension \(m_L\), and assume that \[n - m_L \ge a_0 p^2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad n \le C_1 p^2,\] where \[a_0 = \frac{1}{4} + \epsilon.\] Then, there exists \(\gamma = \gamma(\epsilon) > 0\), such that except on an event of probability at most \[\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma^2 p^2}{4}\right),\] the inequality \[\inf_{D \in \mathcal P_p} \inf_{v \in L} \|(\langle G_i, D\rangle - v_i)_{i \in J}\|_2 \ge\gamma p\] holds simultaneously for all retained row sets \(J \subseteq [n]\) with \(|J| = m\).

Proof

Set \[\Delta = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{\epsilon}{2}} - \frac{1}{2}.\] Choose \(0 < \theta < 1/2\) small enough so that \[\theta \, C_1 \le \frac{\epsilon}{2} \qquad \text{and} \qquad C_1 H_2(\theta) \le \frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{\Delta}{4}\right)^2,\] where \(H_2(t) = -t \log(t) - (1 - t) \log(1 - t)\). This is possible because \(H_2(t) \to 0\) as \(t \downarrow 0\). Define \[\gamma = \frac{\Delta}{4}, k = \lfloor\theta n\rfloor, m = n - k.\]

Lemma 13.4, the dimensional assumptions, and the choice of \(\theta\) give \[r_J = m - \dim L_J \ge n - m_L - k \ge \left(\frac{1}{4} + \frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)p^2.\]

Since, \(\|\cdot\|_{2}\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz, so \(\operatorname{Var}\|g_{r_J}\|_{2} \le 1\) and \(\mathbb E\|g_{r_J}\|_{2}^{2} = r_J\), whence \(\mathbb E\|g_{r_J}\|_{2} \ge \sqrt{r_J - 1}\). Consequently, combining the bounds above with Lemma 13.3, for all sufficiently large \(p\), \[\mathbb E\|g_{r_J}\|_2 - w(\mathcal P_p) \ge 2 \gamma p.\]

Using \(t = \gamma p\) in Lemma 13.5 gives \[\mathbb P\left( \inf_{D \in \mathcal P_p}\inf_{v \in L} \|(\langle G_i, D\rangle - v_i)_{i \in J}\|_2 < \gamma p \right) \le e^{-\gamma^2 p^2 / 2}.\]

There are at most \[\binom{n}{k} \le e^{n H_2(\theta)}\] possible retained sets. Moreover, \[n H_2(\theta) \le C_1 p^2 H_2(\theta) < \frac{\gamma^2 p^2}{4}.\] Hence, except on an event of probability at most \[\exp\left(-\gamma^2 p^2 / 4\right),\] the inequality \[\inf_{D \in \mathcal P_p} \inf_{v \in L} \|(\langle G_i, D\rangle - v_i)_{i \in J}\|_2 \ge\gamma p\] holds simultaneously for all \(J\) with \(|J| = m\).

QED.

Following the notation used in the proof, define the fixed coordinate threshold \[a = \frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{C_1}}.\]

Fix \(D \in \mathcal P_p\) and \(v \in L\). If at most \(k\) residual coordinates \[r_i = \langle G_i, D\rangle - v_i\] satisfied \(|r_i| \ge a\), one could select \(m = n - k\) coordinates with \(|r_i| < a\). On this retained set, \[\|r_J\|_2 < a \sqrt n \le \frac{\gamma p}{2},\] contradicting the simultaneous lower bound. Thus more than \(k\) coordinates satisfy \(|r_i| \ge a\). Since \(k + 1 > \theta n\), their fraction is larger than \(\theta\).

Choose a fixed smooth function \(\chi : [0,\infty) \to [0,1]\) such that \(\chi(s) = 0\) for \(s \le 1/4\) and \(\chi(s) = 1\) for \(s \ge 1\), and put

\[\ell(t) = \chi(t^2/a^2). \tag{13.1}\]

Then \(\ell \in C^3(\mathbb R; [0,1])\), \(\ell(t)=1\) when \(|t| \ge a\), and \[\ell(t) \le C_\ell|t|, \qquad C_\ell = \frac{2}{a}.\] On the preceding Gaussian event,

\[\inf_{D \in \mathcal P_p} \inf_{v \in L} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n \ell(\langle G_i, D\rangle - v_i) \ge \theta. \tag{13.2}\]

This is the required robust Gaussian bounded-loss margin. Every parameter in it depends only on \(\epsilon\) and \(C_1\).

13.5 The empirical Frobenius-to-\(\ell_1\) event

In the previous section we established a lower bound for the worst-case scenario of \(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n \ell(\langle G_i, D\rangle - v_i)\). However, \(G_i\)’s are only covariance-matched isonormal Gaussian versions of \(Z_i\)’s. To start bridging that gap, in this section we provide a uniform \(O(1)\) bound on \(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\langle Z_i, D \rangle|\).

Lemma 13.7.

Define \[\Lambda_p = \sup_{\substack{A \in \operatorname{Sym}_p \\ \|A\|_F = 1}} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\langle Z_i, A \rangle|.\] Then there exist \(A_* = A_*(\epsilon) < \infty\) and a universal constant \(c_2 > 0\), such that \[\mathbb P(\Lambda_p > A_*) \le 2 \exp(-c_2 \, a_0 \, p^2).\]

Proof

For a fixed \(A \in \operatorname{Sym}_p\) with \(\|A\|_F=1\), Hanson-Wright inequality gives \[\mathbb P(|\langle Z_i, A \rangle| > t) \le 2 \exp[-c_0 \min(t^2, t)],\] where \(c_0\) is a universal constant since \(\|A\|_F = 1\) implies that \(\|A\|_{op} \le 1\). Recall that the subexponential norm is defined as \[\|X\|_{\psi_1} = \inf\{t > 0 : \mathbb E\exp(|X| / t) \le 2\}.\] The preceding tail bound implies \(\|\langle Z_i, A \rangle\|_{\psi_1} \le C_0\) uniformly in \(A\), and from Lemma 13.2 and Jensen one has \(\mathbb E|\langle Z_i, A \rangle| \le 1\).

Let \(Y_{i}(A) = |\langle Z_{i}, A \rangle|\). To lighten notation we drop the explicit dependence of \(Y_i(A)\) on \(A\) and just write \(Y_i\). Then \(\bar Y_{i} = Y_{i} - \mathbb EY_{i}\) is centered subexponential with \(\|\bar Y_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}} \le 2 C_{0}\), since centering at most doubles the \(\psi_1\)-norm: \(\|Y - \mathbb EY\|_{\psi_1} \le 2 \|Y\|_{\psi_1}\) because \(|EY| \le \|Y\|_{\psi_1}\). Therefore, Bernstein’s inequality [14, Theorem 2.8.1] gives \[\mathbb P\Bigl(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n \bar Y_{i} > s\Bigr) \le 2 \exp[-c_{1} n \min(s^2, s)]\] with \(c_{1} = c_{1}(C_{0})\). Since \(Y_1, \dots, Y_n\) are i.i.d. and \(\mathbb EY_{1} \le 1\), \[\Bigl\{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n Y_{i} > 1 + s \Bigr\} \subset \Bigl\{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n \bar Y_{i} > s + 1 - \mathbb EY_{1} \Bigr\} \subset \Bigl\{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n \bar Y_{i} > s \Bigr\}.\] Hence \[\mathbb P\left(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n| \langle Z_i, A\rangle| > 1 + s\right) \le 2 \exp[-c_1 n \min(s^2, s)].\]

Let \(d_p = p (p + 1) / 2\). A Frobenius \(1/2\)-net \(\mathcal N\) of the unit sphere in \(\operatorname{Sym}_p\) can be chosen with \[|\mathcal N| \le 5^{d_p}.\] By the assumption of Lemma 13.6, \(n \ge a_0p^2\) and \(a_0 \geq 1/4\), so \[\frac{d_p}{n} \le \frac{p (p + 1)}{2 a_0 p^2} \le \frac{1}{a_0} \le 4,\] hence there exists a universal constant \(c_2 > 0\) and a large enough \(s_0\) depending only on \(\epsilon\) via \(a_0\), such that \[c_1 \min(s_0^2, s_0) - \frac{d_p}{n} \log(5) \ge c_1 \min(s_0^2, s_0) - \frac{\log(5)}{a_0} \ge c_2 > 0.\] The union bound then gives \[\mathbb P\left(\max_{A_0 \in \mathcal N} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\langle Z_i, A_0 \rangle| > 1 + s_0 \right) \le 2 e^{-c_2 n} \le 2 e^{-c_2 a_0 p^2}.\] For any Frobenius-unit \(A\), choose \(A_0 = A_0(A) \in \mathcal N\) with \(\|A - A_0\|_F \le 1/2\). By homogeneity, \[\begin{aligned} \Lambda_p &\le \sup_{\substack{A \in \operatorname{Sym}_p \\ \|A\|_F = 1}} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n \Big(|\langle Z_i, A_0 \rangle| + |\langle Z_i, A - A_0 \rangle|\Big) \\ &\le \max_{A_0' \in \mathcal N} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\langle Z_i, A_0' \rangle| + \frac{1}{2} \Lambda_p, \end{aligned}\] thus, since \(\Lambda_p < \infty\) a.s., \[\{\Lambda_p > 2(1 + s_0)\} \subset\Big\{ \max_{A_0 \in \mathcal N} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\langle Z_i, A_0 \rangle| > 1 + s_0 \Big\}.\] Taking \(A_* = 2 (1 + s_0)\), depending only on \(\epsilon\) through \(s_0\), one gets \[\mathbb P(\Lambda_p > A_*) \le 2e^{-c_2 a_0 p^2}.\]

QED.

13.6 Discretization and an approximation to the minimum

Section 13.4 gives a uniform positive lower bound for the Gaussian model, and Lemma 13.7 gives that, with high probability, \(\Lambda_p \le A_*\). Both are infima/suprema over a continuum with a non-smooth extremum. This section replaces the continuum by a finite collection and the extremum by a smooth average suitable for interpolation. We show that both the discretization and smoothing errors can be made small, both a small fraction of the Gaussian margin with fixed constants.

For the moment keep \(\tau \in (0,1]\) symbolic, and let \[\mathcal K_\tau = \{D \in {\rm Sym}_p : D \succeq 0, \|D\|_F = 1, \|D\|_{op} \le \tau\}.\] If this class is empty, Theorem 13.1 is immediate. Assume below that it is nonempty, forcing \(\tau \geq 1 / \sqrt{p}\).

Fix \(0 < B < \infty\) and let \[\mathcal V = L\cap\{v : \|v\|_1 \le B n\}\] be the radius-\((B n)\) ball in the \(m_L\)-dimensional normed space \((L,\|\cdot\|_1)\).

Lemma 13.8.

For \(0 < r \le 1\), \(0 < s \le B\) there is a maximal \(r\)-net \(\mathcal N_D \subset {\mathcal K}_\tau\) in Frobenius norm with centers in \({\mathcal K}_\tau\) and an \((s n)\)-net \(\mathcal N_L \subset \mathcal V\) in \(\ell_1\)-norm intrinsic to \(L\) such that for \(\mathcal C = \mathcal N_D \times \mathcal N_L\), \[\begin{aligned} \frac{\log(|\mathcal C|)}{n} \le R_*, \end{aligned}\] where \[R_* = \frac{1}{a_0} \log\left(\frac{3}{r}\right) + \log \Bigl(1 + \frac{2 B}{s}\Bigr).\]

Proof

\({\mathcal K}_\tau\) lies in the Frobenius unit ball of dimension \(d_p\). A maximal \(r\)-net has disjoint \(r / 2\)-balls, volume comparison gives \[|\mathcal N_D| (r / 2)^{d_p} \le (1 + r / 2)^{d_p},\] i.e., \[\log(|\mathcal N_D|) \le d_p \log(1 + 2 / r) \le d_p \log(3 / r) \qquad \text{for $r \le 1$}.\]

\(\mathcal V\) is a \((B n)\)-ball in \((L , \|\cdot\|_1)\), covered by \((1 + 2 B / s)^{m_L}\) balls of radius \((s n)\), thus \[\log(|\mathcal N_L|) \le m_L \log\left(1 + \frac{2 B}{s}\right).\]

Therefore, \[\begin{aligned} \log(|\mathcal C|) \le d_p \log\left(\frac{3}{r}\right) + m_L \log\Bigl(1 + \frac{2 B}{s}\Bigr). \end{aligned}\]

Since \(m_L \leq n\) and Lemma 13.6 implies that \(d_p \le p^2\) and \(n \ge a_0 p^2\), the claim follows.

QED.

A candidate \(u = (R_u, v_u) \in \mathcal C\) has row shift \(c_{iu} = -(v_u)_i\). For a row ensemble \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\), define the total bounded loss \[H_X(u) = \sum_{i = 1}^n \ell(\langle X_i, R_u \rangle + c_{iu})\] and its normalized log-sum-exp approximation \[F_X = -\frac{1}{\beta n} \log\left[\frac{1}{|\mathcal C|} \sum_{u \in \mathcal C} e^{-\beta H_X(u)} \right].\]

Lemma 13.9.

Let \(H_* = \min_u H_X(u)\), \(L_j = \|\ell^{(j)}\|_\infty\), choose the mesh radii \[r \le \min\left\{1, \frac{\theta}{64 L_1 A_*}\right\} \qquad \text{and} \qquad s \le \min\left\{B, \frac{\theta}{64 L_1}\right\},\] and \(\beta \ge (16 R_*) / \theta\). Then \[\frac{H_*}{n} \le F_X \le \frac{H_*}{n} + \frac{\theta}{16}.\]

Proof

We have \[\frac{e^{-\beta H_*}}{|\mathcal C|} \le \frac{1}{|\mathcal C|} \sum_u e^{-\beta H_X(u)} \le e^{-\beta H_*}.\] Taking \(-(\beta n)^{-1}\) times the logarithm proves the exact inequalities \[\frac{H_*}{n} \le F_X \le \frac{H_*}{n} + \frac{\log(|\mathcal C|)}{\beta n}.\] Then \[\frac{\log|\mathcal C|}{\beta n} \le \frac{\theta}{16},\]

and the claim follows.

QED.

The order is important: first choose the Gaussian margin and \(\ell\), then the two fixed mesh radii, and then the fixed \(\beta\). The covering estimate is uniform in \(\tau\). The telescoping argument in Section 13.11 chooses \(\tau\) after \(\beta\), and the net is then instantiated inside that chosen class.

13.7 One conditional Gibbs law shared by both current-row distributions

Let \(G_1, \ldots, G_n\) be independent isonormal Gaussian rows, independent of \(Z_1, \ldots, Z_n\). Define \[X^{(m)} = (Z_1, \ldots, Z_m, G_{m + 1}, \ldots, G_n) \qquad \text{for $0 \le m \le n$}.\] Then \(X^{(n)}=Z\), \(X^{(0)}=G\), and \[\mathbb E[F_Z] - \mathbb E[F_G] = \sum_{m = 1}^n \mathbb E[F_{X^{(m)}} - F_{X^{(m - 1)}}].\] Fix \(m\) and set

\[\mathcal F_{-m} = \sigma(Z_1, \ldots, Z_{m - 1}, G_{m + 1}, \ldots, G_n).\] The rows \(Z_m\) and \(G_m\) are mutually independent and are both independent of \(\mathcal F_{-m}\). Every hybrid row other than the \(m\)-th is \(\mathcal F_{-m}\)-measurable. Define the row-deleted Gibbs distribution on the net \(\mathcal C = \mathcal N_D\times\mathcal N_L\). For \(u = (R_u, v_u) \in \mathcal C\), \(c_{iu} = -(v_u)_i\), \[\nu_{-m}(u) = \frac{e^{-\beta \sum_{i \ne m} \ell(\langle X_i, R_u \rangle + c_{iu})}}{\sum_{v \in \mathcal C} e^{-\beta \sum_{i \ne m} \ell(\langle X_i, R_v \rangle + c_{iv})}}.\] Because the sum excludes \(i=m\), \(\nu_{-m}\) is \(\mathcal F_{-m}\)-measurable.

The other hybrid rows are identical on both sides of the replacement. Therefore the same conditional law governs both replacements; no Gibbs object depending on either current row is used in the conditioning step.

Let \[H_{-m}(u) = \sum_{i \ne m} \ell(\langle X_i, R_u \rangle + c_{iu}),\] where \(X_i\) is the common \(X_i^{(m)} = X_i^{(m - 1)}\). Then \[\sum_u e^{-\beta H_X(u)} = \left(\sum_v e^{-\beta H_{-m}(v)}\right) \cdot \sum_u \nu_{-m}(u) e^{-\beta\ell(\langle X_m, R_u \rangle + c_{mu})}\]

Hence, \[F_X = C_{-m} + \phi_{\nu}(X_m)\] where \[C_{-m} = -\frac{1}{\beta n} \log\sum_v e^{-\beta H_{-m}(v)} + \frac{1}{\beta n} \log|\mathcal C|\] is \(\mathcal F_{-m}\)-measurable and independent of \(X_m\), and \[\phi_{\nu}(X) = -\frac{1}{\beta n} \log\sum_{u \in \mathcal C} \nu_{-m}(u) e^{-\beta\ell(\langle X, R_u \rangle + c_{mu})}.\]

\(C_{-m}\) cancels in the difference: \[F_{X^{(m)}} - F_{X^{(m - 1)}} = \phi_{\nu}(Z_m) - \phi_{\nu}(G_m).\] Therefore, \[\mathbb E[F_{X^{(m)}}-F_{X^{(m-1)}}] = \mathbb E\big[ \mathbb E[\phi_{\nu}(Z_m)-\phi_{\nu}(G_m)\mid\mathcal F_{-m}]\big].\]

Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{-m}\), \(\nu_{-m}\) is deterministic, \(Z_m\) and \(G_m\) are independent of it and have matching first two moments. The \(m\)-th summand is now a one-row comparison problem.

13.8 Exact derivatives and the independent-draw expansion

For a generic current row \(X\), define \[q_u = \langle X, R_u \rangle + c_{mu}.\] Let \(\mu_X\) be the full Gibbs law obtained by tilting \(\nu_{-m}\) with \(e^{-\beta\ell(q_u)}\). Relative to the leave-one-row law, it is \[\frac{d\mu_X}{d\nu_{-m}}(u) = \frac{e^{-\beta \ell(q_u)}}{\sum_v \nu_{-m}(v) e^{-\beta \ell(q_v)}}.\] Write \[\langle f \rangle_X = \sum_{u \in \mathcal C} f(u) \mu_X(u)\] and \[\operatorname{Cov}_X(f, g) = \langle f g \rangle_X - \langle f \rangle_X \langle g \rangle_X.\]

For a row direction \(A \in \operatorname{Sym}_p\), put \[\begin{aligned} a_A(u) &= \ell'(q_u) \langle A, R_u \rangle, \\ b_{AB}(u) &= \ell''(q_u) \langle A, R_u \rangle \langle B, R_u \rangle, \\ c_{ABC}(u) &= \ell'''(q_u) \langle A, R_u \rangle \langle B, R_u \rangle \langle C, R_u \rangle. \end{aligned}\] For every row-dependent observable \(f\), \[D\langle f\rangle_X[B] = \langle D f[B] \rangle_X - \beta\operatorname{Cov}_X(f, a_B).\] The first term is differentiation inside, the second is differentiation of the Gibbs weight itself. Differentiating gives \[\begin{aligned} D\phi_\nu(X)[A] &= \frac{1}{n} \langle a_A \rangle_X, \\ D^2\phi_\nu(X)[A,B] &= \frac{1}{n} \left( \langle b_{AB} \rangle_X - \beta[\langle a_A a_B \rangle_X - \langle a_A \rangle_X \langle a_B \rangle_X] \right). \end{aligned}\] Let \(\sum_{cyc}\) denote the three cyclic choices of \((A, B, C)\). Then \[n D^3\phi_\nu(X)[A, B, C] = \langle c_{ABC} \rangle_X - \beta\sum_{cyc} \operatorname{Cov}_X(b_{A B}, a_C) + \beta^2\kappa_{3, X}(a_A, a_B, a_C),\] where \[\kappa_{3,X}(a, b, c) = \langle abc \rangle_X - \langle ab \rangle_X \langle c \rangle_X - \langle ac \rangle_X \langle b \rangle_X - \langle bc \rangle_X \langle a \rangle_X + 2 \langle a \rangle_X \langle b \rangle_X \langle c \rangle_X.\] The \(\ell'''\) group is the one-independent-draw from the same Gibbs probability measure term \[\langle c_{ABC}\rangle_X.\] For each of the three cyclic choices, the \(\ell'' \ell'\) group is \[\operatorname{Cov}_X(b_{A B}, a_C) = \langle b_{A B} a_C \rangle_X -\langle b_{A B} \rangle_X \langle a_C \rangle_X,\] which is respectively a one-independent-draw term and a two-independent-draw term. The \((\ell')^3\) group has one one-independent-draw term, \(\langle a_A a_B a_C\rangle_X\), three two-independent-draw terms \[-\langle a_A a_B \rangle_X \langle a_C \rangle_X, -\langle a_A a_C \rangle_X \langle a_B \rangle_X, -\langle a_B a_C \rangle_X \langle a_A \rangle_X,\] and the three-independent-draw term \[2 \langle a_A \rangle_X \langle a_B \rangle_X \langle a_C \rangle_X.\]

Since \(0 \le \ell \le 1\) by (13.1), \[e^{-\beta} \le \frac{d\mu_X}{d\nu_{-m}} \le e^\beta.\] For product measures (one, two, and three independent draws) the exact product bounds are: \[e^{-k \beta} \le \frac{d\mu_X^{\otimes k}}{d\nu_{-m}^{\otimes k}} \le e^{k \beta} \qquad \text{for $k = 1, 2, 3$}.\]

If we replace an expectation under \(\mu_X\) by one under \(\nu_{-m}\) we pay at most \(e^{k\beta}\). Only after these replacements do we take absolute values and use \[|\ell'|\le L_1, |\ell''|\le L_2, |\ell'''|\le L_3.\] After this change of measure, independent samples are drawn from \(\nu_{-m}\), which is \({\mathcal F}_{-m}\)-measurable and independent of both \(Z_m\) and \(G_m\). Its \(R_u\)’s are fixed given \({\mathcal F}_{-m}\).

Then the scalar part of \(D^3\) can be bounded purely by constants. The \(R\)-dependent part \(\langle A,R_{u_1} \rangle \langle B, R_{u_2} \rangle \langle C, R_{u_3}\rangle\) remains, but now \(u_i\) are independent of \(X\).

Counting absolute values: \(Cov\) gives 2 terms per cyclic, \(\kappa_3\) gives \(1+1+1+1+2=6\) terms in absolute value. Hence the total absolute scalar mass is at most \[\frac{1}{n} \big[L_3 + 6 \beta L_1 L_2 + 6 \beta^2 L_1^3\big].\]

13.9 An exact integration-by-parts identity for centered rank-one rows

The next identity is the only point where the non-Gaussian distribution of \(Z=(ww^\top - I_p)/\sqrt2\) enters. We derive it in coordinates rather than invoking a named interpolation principle. The Hessian term matches the Gaussian row exactly; the remaining error contains the third derivative and is small when all candidate matrices have small operator norm.

For \(1\le a, b \le p\), define \[S_{ab} = \frac{e_a e_b^\top + e_b e_a^\top}{2}\] and \[B_a(w) = \frac{e_a w^\top + w e_a^\top}{\sqrt{2}}.\] Then \[\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w_a} = B_a(w)\] and \[\frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w_a \partial w_b} = \sqrt{2} S_{ab}.\] Let \(M \in \operatorname{Sym}_p\) be independent of \(w\), let \(s\ge0\) be deterministic, and set \(Y = M + s Z\). Consider \(\Phi\in C^3(\operatorname{Sym}_p)\) with bounded derivatives, and identify \(D\Phi\) with the symmetric gradient and \(D^2\Phi\), \(D^3\Phi\) with the symmetric bi/tri-linear forms.

Let \[f_{ab}(w) = \langle D\Phi(Y), S_{ab} \rangle.\] Note that since \(D^j\Phi\) is bounded for \(j = 1, 2, 3\), it follows that \(f_{ab}\) is bounded, and \(\partial_a\partial_b f_{ab}\) is at most quadratic in \(w\). Since \(M\) is independent of \(w\), the requirements of conditional Stein’s lemma, given \(M\), are met; and by the law of total expectation, \[\mathbb E[(w_a w_b - \delta_{ab}) f_{ab}(w)] = \mathbb E[\partial_a \partial_b f_{ab}(w)].\]

Since \[\langle Z, D\Phi(Y) \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_{a,b} (w_a w_b - \delta_{ab}) \langle D\Phi(Y), S_{ab} \rangle,\] the chain rule gives, before summing over \(a, b\), \[\partial_a\partial_b f_{ab}(w) = s^2 D^3\Phi(Y)[B_a(w), B_b(w), S_{ab}] + s \sqrt{2} D^2\Phi(Y)[S_{ab}, S_{ab}].\] Indeed, \(\partial_a Y = s B_a(w)\) and \(\partial_a B_b(w) = \sqrt{2} S_{ab}\). Multiplication by \(1/\sqrt{2}\) and summation over the ordered pairs \((a, b)\) therefore gives \[\mathbb E\langle Z, D\Phi(M + s Z) \rangle = s \, \mathbb E\Delta\Phi(M + s Z) + s^2 \, \mathbb E\mathfrak T_w(D^3\Phi(M + sZ)),\] where \[\Delta\Phi(Y) = \operatorname{Tr} D^2 \Phi(Y) = \sum_{a \leq b} D^2\Phi(Y)[Q_{ab}, Q_{ab}]\] and \[\mathfrak T_w(T) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \sum_{a, b = 1}^p T[B_a(w), B_b(w), S_{ab}].\] The Hessian term is exactly the Frobenius Laplacian. Indeed, \(S_{aa} = Q_{aa}\), while \(S_{ab} = Q_{ab}/\sqrt{2}\) for \(a \ne b\), and the two ordered pairs \((a, b)\) and \((b, a)\) give one copy of \(D^2\Phi[Q_{ab}, Q_{ab}]\).

For matrices \(A,B,C \in \operatorname{Sym}_p\), the ordered pure tensor \(T = A \otimes B \otimes C\) is the trilinear form \[T[U,V,W] = \langle A, U \rangle \langle B, V \rangle \langle C, W \rangle.\] For this tensor, \[\mathfrak T_w(A \otimes B \otimes C) = \sqrt{2} w^\top A C B w.\] This follows from \[\langle A, B_a(w) \rangle = \sqrt{2}(A w)_a\] and \(\langle C, S_{ab} \rangle = C_{ab}\).

For \(\Phi=\phi_\nu\), finiteness of the candidate family and boundedness of the first three derivatives of \(\ell\) give deterministic bounds on \(D^j\Phi\) for \(1 \le j \le 3\). The Gaussian variables appearing below have finite moments of every order. Hence, on every interval \([\delta, 1 - \delta]\), the difference quotients and integration-by-parts integrands admit an integrable dominating function, which justifies differentiating under the expectation. The bound obtained below is uniform in \(\delta\) and justifies passage to the endpoints by dominated convergence.

Now set \[Y_t = \sqrt{t} \, G + \sqrt{1 - t} \, Z.\] Gaussian integration by parts gives \[\mathbb E\langle G, D\Phi(Y_t) \rangle = \sqrt{t} \, \mathbb E\Delta\Phi(Y_t).\] Using the second-chaos identity with \(s = \sqrt{1 - t}\) yields \[\frac d{dt}\mathbb E\Phi(Y_t) = -\frac{\sqrt{1 - t}}{2} \, \mathbb E\mathfrak T_w(D^3\Phi(Y_t)).\] The right side is uniformly bounded and tends to zero as \(t \uparrow 1\). The identity can first be integrated on \([\delta, 1 - \delta]\) and then \(\delta \downarrow 0\). Thus both endpoints are integrable, and no fourth derivative is used.

13.10 Bounding the third-derivative error for one replaced row

Let \(R_1, R_2, R_3\) be symmetric and satisfy \[\|R_j\|_F \le 1, \|R_j\|_{op} \le \tau \le 1.\] For every ordering, \[|\operatorname{Tr}(R_1 R_2 R_3)| \le \|R_1\|_F \|R_2 R_3\|_F \le \tau\] and \[\|R_1 R_2 R_3\|_F \le \|R_1\|_{op} \|R_2\|_{op} \|R_3\|_F \le \tau^2.\] The symmetric part \(Q\) of the ordered product obeys the same bounds. Since \[\mathbb E|w^\top Q w| \le |\operatorname{Tr}Q| + [\mathbb E|w^\top Q w - \operatorname{Tr}Q|^2]^{1/2},\] and \[\mathbb E|w^\top Q w - \operatorname{Tr}Q|^2 = 2\|Q\|_F^2,\] we obtain \[\mathbb E|w^\top Q w| \le \tau + \sqrt{2} \tau^2 \le (1 + \sqrt{2}) \tau.\] Combining this with the termwise change-of-measure bounds and the tensor contraction gives a universal \(C_2\) such that \[\mathbb E|\mathfrak T_w(D^3\phi_\nu(Y_t))| \le \frac{C_2 e^{3\beta}\tau}{n} [L_3 + \beta L_1 L_2 + \beta^2 L_1^3].\] Integrating the interpolation and absorbing the constant factors into \(C_2\) gives the normalized one-row estimate \[|\mathbb E\phi_\nu(Z) - \mathbb E\phi_\nu(G)| \le \frac{C_2 e^{3\beta} \tau}{n} [L_3 + \beta L_1 L_2 + \beta^2 L_1^3].\] The factor \(1/n\) comes from the free-energy normalization. The estimate is uniform in the leave-one-row law, the finite family, the shifts, \(p\), and \(n\).

13.11 Telescoping, concentration, and continuum extension

Summing the \(n\) hybrid-row estimates yields \[|\mathbb EF_Z - \mathbb EF_G| \le C_2 e^{3\beta} \tau [L_3 + \beta L_1 L_2 + \beta^2 L_1^3].\] Choose \(\tau \le 1\) after \(\beta\) so that the right side is at most \(\theta / 16\). Then for \(p \ge \tau^{-2}\), \(1/\sqrt p \le \tau\) and \({\mathcal K}_\tau \neq \emptyset\). So a \(p\)-independent \(\tau\) works for all sufficiently large \(p\).

On the Gaussian robust event, every candidate has \(H_G(u) / n \ge \theta\). Since \(F_G \ge \min_u H_G(u) / n\) and \(F_G \ge 0\) always, \[\mathbb EF_G \ge \theta[1 - e^{-\gamma^2 p^2 / 4}].\] For all sufficiently large \(p\), this is at least \(15 \theta / 16\). Hence \[\mathbb EF_Z \ge \frac{7 \theta}{8}.\] Replacing one row changes every \(H_X(u)\) by at most one.

The log-sum-exp map therefore changes \(F_X\) by at most \(1 / n\). The bounded-differences inequality [8] gives \[\mathbb P(|F_Z - \mathbb EF_Z| > t) \le 2 e^{-2 n t^2}.\]

With \(t = \theta / 8\), \[\mathbb P\left(F_Z < \frac{3 \theta}{4}\right) \le 2 e^{-n \theta^2 / 32}.\] The free-energy inequality and the entropy choice imply, on the complement of this event, \[\min_{u \in \mathcal C} \frac{H_Z(u)} n \ge F_Z - \frac{\log|\mathcal C|}{\beta n} \ge \frac{11 \theta}{16}.\]

Now take any \(D \in \mathcal K_\tau\) and \(v\in L\) with \(\|v\|_1 \le B n\). Choose net points \(R\) and \(v_0\) satisfying \[\|D - R\|_F \le r, \|v - v_0\|_1 \le s n.\] If \(D = R\) set \(A = 0\), otherwise put \(A = (D - R) / \|D - R\|_F\) so \(\|A\|_F = 1\) and \(D - R = \|D - R\|_F A\). Then it follows from the definition of \(\Lambda_p\) that \[\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\langle Z_i, D - R \rangle| = \|D - R\|_F \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\langle Z_i, A\rangle| \le \|D - R\|_F \Lambda_p \le r \Lambda_p\] on \(\{\Lambda_p \le A_*\}\), with the convention \(0 \cdot \Lambda_p = 0\). In particular on \(\{\Lambda_p \le A_*\}\), \[\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n \big(|\langle Z_i, D - R \rangle| + |v_i - (v_0)_i|\big) \le \Lambda_p r + s \le A_*r + s.\] Therefore the Lipschitz property of \(\ell\) gives \[\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n |\ell(\langle Z_i, D \rangle - v_i) - \ell(\langle Z_i, R \rangle - (v_0)_i)| \le L_1(A_* r + s) \le \frac{\theta}{32},\] hence \[\inf_{\substack{D \in \mathcal K_\tau \\ v \in L, \|v\|_1 \le B n}} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i = 1}^n \ell(\langle Z_i, D \rangle - v_i) \ge \frac{\theta}{2}.\] Since \(\ell(t) \le C_\ell |t|\), the conclusion of Theorem 13.1 holds with \[c = \frac\theta{2 C_\ell}.\] For large \(p\), the total failure probability is at most \[2 e^{-n \theta^2 / 32} + 2 e^{-c_2' n} \le e^{-c'p^2}\] after decreasing \(c' > 0\), because \(n \ge a_0 p^2\) and \(n \le C_1 p^2\).

This proves Theorem 13.1.

13.12 Conditional nuisance-space reduction

Fix a deterministic subspace \(E_0\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) of dimension \(j\le K\), and split each sample as \[x_i=g_i+w_i\in E_0\oplus E_0^\perp.\]

Suppose that \[S_0=H_0+\rho D_0,\] where \(H_0\) is supported on \(E_0\), \(D_0\) is supported on \(E_0^\perp\), \[D_0\succeq0,\qquad \|D_0\|_F=1,\qquad \|D_0\|_{op}\le\tau,\] and \(\rho\ge\delta\).

Let \[\mathcal G=\sigma(g_1,\ldots,g_n).\] Conditional on \(\mathcal G\), the vectors \(w_i\) are still independent \(N(0,I_p)\) vectors with \(p=d-j\). Define \[\mathcal Q_w(A)_i=\frac{w_i^TAw_i-\operatorname{Tr}A} {\sqrt2} =\langle Z_i,A\rangle.\] For \(A\in\operatorname{Sym}(E_0)\), also define \[\mathcal Q_g(A)_i=\frac{g_i^TAg_i-\operatorname{Tr}A} {\sqrt2}.\] Now define \[L(E_0,g)=\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol 1_n\}+\{\mathcal Q_g(A): A\in\operatorname{Sym}(E_0)\}.\] This is a \(\mathcal G\)-measurable subspace. After conditioning, it is a deterministic subspace independent of all tail rows, and \[\dim L(E_0,g)\le1+\frac{j(j+1)}2\le1+\frac{K(K+1)}2.\] For every realization of \(g\) for which the dimensional inequality holds, define \[\mathcal E_{\rm str}(g)=\left\{ \inf_{\substack{D\succeq0,\ \|D\|_F=1,\ \|D\|_{op}\le\tau\\ v\in L(E_0,g),\ \|v\|_1\le Bn}} \frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D)-v\|_1\ge c \right\}.\] Then Theorem 13.1 and the empirical continuity estimate give \[\mathbb P\left((\mathcal E_{\rm str}(g)\cap\{\Lambda_w\le A_*\})^c\mid\mathcal G\right)\le3e^{-c'p^2},\] where \[\Lambda_w=\sup_{\|A\|_F=1}\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(A)\|_1.\] The bound is independent of the orientation of the realized nuisance subspace. Integrating over \(g\) preserves the same bound. Since the event in Theorem 13.1 takes an infimum over the full classes of \(D\) and \(v\), both may be measurable functions of the tail rows. This is why the nuisance vector may be selected after observing the tail.

The proof of Theorem 13.1 also gives the joint tail-continuity event \[\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(A)\|_1\le A_*\|A\|_F\] for every symmetric \(A\), with conditional failure probability at most \(e^{-cp^2}\). In particular, \[\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)\|_1\le A_*.\]

For the decomposition \(S_0=H_0+\rho D_0\), define \[\bar a = \frac1{\rho n}\boldsymbol 1_n^T\mathcal Q_d(S_0), \qquad v = -\mathcal Q_g(H_0/\rho)+\bar a\boldsymbol 1_n.\] Then \[\mathcal Q_w(D_0)-v = \rho^{-1}\Pi_n\mathcal Q_d(S_0).\]

Assume \(B>A_*+1\), and decrease \(c\) if necessary so that \(0<c\le1\). On \[\mathcal E_{\rm str}(g)\cap\{\Lambda_w\le A_*\},\] if \[\frac1n\|\Pi_n\mathcal Q_d(S_0)\|_1<\rho c,\] then \[\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)-v\|_1<c.\] Moreover, \[\frac1n\|v\|_1 \le \frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)\|_1 + \frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)-v\|_1 < A_*+1 < B.\] This contradicts the definition of \(\mathcal E_{\rm str}(g)\). Therefore, on the same good event, \[\frac1n\|\Pi_n\mathcal Q_d(S_0)\|_1 \ge \rho c \ge \delta c.\]

Thus the conditional diffuse margin holds uniformly for every deterministic \(E_0\) of dimension at most \(K\), with conditional failure probability at most \(3e^{-c'p^2}\).

Disjoint Frobenius balls of radius \(\zeta/4\) around these frames lie in the Euclidean ball of radius \(\sqrt j+\zeta/4\) in \(\mathbb R^{dj}\), which proves the displayed cardinality. Maximality makes the set a \(\zeta/2\)-net, and \[\|UU^{T}-VV^{T}\|_{op}\le2\|U-V\|_{F}\] shows that the corresponding subspaces form a \(\zeta\)- net.

For the last claim, rotate the principal two-planes from \(E\) to \(F\) and act as the identity on the remaining directions. If \(\theta_{\max}\) is the largest principal angle, then \[\sin\theta_{\max}=\|P_E-P_F\|_{op},\qquad \|O-I\|_{op}=2\sin(\theta_{\max}/2) \le\sqrt2\sin \theta_{\max},\] since \(\zeta<1/2\) implies \(\theta_{\max}<\pi/2\) and \(\cos(\theta_{\max}/2)\ge1/\sqrt2\).

QED.

Theorem 16.4 (Uniform conic lower bound).

For fixed \(1/4<\alpha_-\le\alpha_+<\infty\), there are \(c,C>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(d\), whenever \[\alpha_-d^2\le n\le\alpha_+d^2,\] one has \[\mathbb P\left[ \inf_{\substack{S\succeq0\\\|S\|_F=1}} \frac1n\|\Pi_n\mathcal{Q}_d(S)\|_1<c \right]\le e^{-Cd^2}.\]

Proof

Set \[\epsilon_0=\frac12\left(\alpha_--\frac14\right)\text{ and }C_1=4\alpha_+.\] Let \(C_{\rm cont}\) be the global continuity constant for the aspect interval \([\alpha_-,\alpha_+]\), let \(c_*\) be the fixed-rank constant, and let \(A_*=A_*(\epsilon_0,C_1)\) be the tail-continuity constant proved in Section 13. Choose \(0<\delta<1/2\) so that \[g_{\rm lr}=c_*\sqrt{1-\delta^2}-2C_{\rm cont}\delta>0,\] and set \(B=A_*+2\). Apply Theorem 15.1 with parameters \((\epsilon_0,B,C_1)\), and denote its margin and probability constants by \(c_{\rm str}\) and \(c'_{\rm str}\). Let \(\tau\) be its operator-norm cap, and then choose \(K=K(\tau,\delta)\) by Lemma 16.2.

By the preceding Grassmann cover and rotation statement, the rotation below satisfies \(\|O-I\|_{op}\le C_O\zeta\) with \(C_O=\sqrt2\). Put \(C_{\rm rot}=2C_OC_{\rm cont}\) and choose a fixed \(\zeta>0\) such that \[C_{\rm rot}\frac\zeta\delta\le\min\left\{ \frac{c_{\rm str}}4,\frac12\right\}.\] Finally choose \[0<c_{\rm cone}<\min\left\{\frac{g_{\rm lr}}2, \frac{\delta c_{\rm str}}4,\frac\delta2\right\}.\] In the low-rank branch, if \(n^{-1}\|\Pi_n\mathcal{Q}_d (S)\|_1<c_{\rm cone}\), then \[c_*\|H\|_F \le\frac1n\|\Pi_n\mathcal{Q}_d(H)\|_1 \le\frac1n\|\Pi_n\mathcal{Q}_d(S)\|_1+2C_{\rm cont} \|R\|_F.\] Since \(\|H\|_F\ge\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\), this gives \[c_*\sqrt{1-\delta^2}<c_{\rm cone}+2C_{\rm cont}\delta< c_*\sqrt{1-\delta^2},\] a contradiction.

In the diffuse branch, let \(j=\operatorname{rank}H\) and \(E=\operatorname{range}H\). For each \(0\le j\le K\), fix a deterministic \(\zeta\)-net of \(\operatorname{Gr}(j,d)\) before observing the samples. Choose the nearest point \(E_0\) and an orthogonal \(O\) such that \[OE=E_0\text{ and }\|O-I\|_{op}\le C_O\zeta.\] Put \[S_0=OSO^T=H_0+\rho D_0.\] Then \(H_0\) is supported on \(E_0\), \(D_0\) on \(E_0^\perp\), and the canonical rotation obeys the following sharper PSD estimate. If \(S=\sum_j\lambda_jv_jv_j^T\) with \(\lambda_j\ge0\), then \[\operatorname{Tr}(SOSO^T) =\sum_{j,k}\lambda_j\lambda_k (v_j^TOv_k)^2 \ge\cos^2(\theta_{\max})\sum_j\lambda_j^2,\] because the symmetric part of the canonical principal-plane rotation is at least \(\cos(\theta_{\max})I\). Consequently, \[\|OSO^T-S\|_F^2 =2\operatorname{Tr}(S^2)-2\operatorname {Tr}(SOSO^T) \le2\sin^2(\theta_{\max})\|S\|_F^2.\] Since \(\|S\|_F=1\) and \(\sin\theta_{\max}\le\zeta\), this gives \[\|S_0-S\|_F\le C_O\zeta\text{ and }\frac{\|S_0-S\|_F} {\rho}\le C_O\frac\zeta\delta.\] For fixed \(E_0\), write \(x_i=g_i+w_i\) along \(E_0\oplus E_0^\perp\) and define \[\mathcal G_{E_0}=\sigma(g_1,\ldots,g_n).\] Conditional on \(\mathcal G_{E_0}\), the \(w_i\) are independent \(N(0,I_p)\) vectors with \(p=d-j\). For \(A\in\operatorname{Sym}(E_0)\) and \(D\in\operatorname{Sym}(E_0^\perp)\), define \[\mathcal Q_g(A)_i=\frac{g_i^TAg_i-\operatorname{Tr}A} {\sqrt2}\text{ and } \mathcal Q_w(D)_i= \frac{w_i^TDw_i-\operatorname{Tr}D}{\sqrt2}.\] Then \[\mathcal Q_d(H_0+\rho D_0)=\mathcal Q_g(H_0)+\rho \mathcal Q_w(D_0).\] Define \[L(E_0,g)=\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol 1_n\}+\{\mathcal Q_g(A): A\in\operatorname{Sym}(E_0)\}.\] After conditioning, this is a deterministic subspace independent of the tail samples, and \[\dim L(E_0,g)\le1+\frac{j(j+1)}2.\] The tail dimension is \(p=d-j=d-O_K(1)\) and the nuisance dimension is \(O_K(1)\). For all sufficiently large \(d\), \[n-\dim L(E_0,g)\ge\alpha_-d^2-1-\frac{K(K+1)}2\ge \left(\frac14+\epsilon_0\right)p^2,\] and, because \(p\ge d/2\), \[n\le\alpha_+d^2\le4\alpha_+p^2=C_1p^2.\] Put \[\bar a=\frac1{\rho n}\boldsymbol 1_n^T\mathcal Q_d(S_0)\text{ and } v=-\mathcal Q_g(H_0/\rho)+\bar a\boldsymbol 1_n.\] The block identity and the definition of \(\Pi_n\) give \[\mathcal Q_w(D_0)-v=\rho^{-1}\Pi_n\mathcal Q_d(S_0).\] On the global continuity event, \(\|\Pi_nz\|_1\le2 \|z\|_1\) and the rotation estimate give \[\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)-v\|_1 \le C_{\rm rot} \frac\zeta\delta+\frac1{\delta n}\|\Pi_n\mathcal Q_d(S)\|_1.\] Conditional on \(\mathcal G_{E_0}\), define \[\mathcal E_{\rm str}=\left\{ \inf_{\substack{D\succeq0,\ \|D\|_F=1,\ \|D\|_{op}\le\tau\\ v\in L(E_0,g),\ \|v\|_1\le Bn}} \frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D)-v\|_1\ge c_{\rm str} \right\}.\] Then Theorem 15.1 and the tail-continuity estimate from Section 13 give, for a constant \(c_{\rm cond}>0\) depending only on the fixed parameters, \[\mathbb P\left((\mathcal E_{\rm str}\cap\{\Lambda_w\le A_*\})^c\mid\mathcal G_{E_0}\right)\le3e^{-c_{\rm cond}p^2},\] where \[\Lambda_w=\sup_{\|D\|_F=1}\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D)\|_1.\] The bound is uniform in the realized nuisance subspace.

Suppose that \(n^{-1}\|\Pi_n\mathcal Q_d(S)\|_1< c_{\rm cone}\). On the preceding good events, \[\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)-v\|_1 \le C_{\rm rot} \frac\zeta\delta+\frac{c_{\rm cone}}\delta \le \frac{c_{\rm str}}2.\] Moreover, \[\frac1n\|v\|_1 \le\frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)\|_1+ \frac1n\|\mathcal Q_w(D_0)-v\|_1 \le A_*+1<B.\] Thus \(D_0\) and \(v\) lie in the classes prohibited by Theorem 15.1, a contradiction. Uniformity over both classes permits \(D_0\) and \(v\) to be selected after observing the tail rows.

The deterministic Grassmann nets constructed above satisfy \[\sum_{j=0}^K N(\operatorname{Gr}(j,d),\zeta) \le1+ \sum_{j=1}^K \left(1+\frac{4\sqrt j}{\zeta}\right)^{dj} \le(K+1)e^{C_{K,\zeta}d}.\] Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(d\), \[(K+1)e^{C_{K,\zeta}d}3e^{-c_{\rm cond}(d-K)^2} \le e^{-c_{\rm prob}d^2}\] for a fixed \(c_{\rm prob}>0\). Adding the global-continuity and fixed-rank exceptional events proves the theorem with margin \(c=c_{\rm cone}\) and a positive failure exponent depending only on \(\alpha_-\) and \(\alpha_+\).

If \(T\in\mathcal F_d\), then \(T\ne0\). The normalization \(S=T/\|T\|_F\) satisfies \(S\succeq0\), \(\|S\|_F=1\), and \(\Pi_n\mathcal Q_d(S)=0\), contradicting the margin. If \(\liminf n/d^2>1/4\) but the aspect ratio is unbounded, choose a fixed \(\alpha_0\) with \[\frac14<\alpha_0<\min\left\{\frac12, \liminf_d\frac{n}{d^2}\right\}.\] For all sufficiently large \(d\), \(\lfloor\alpha_0d^2\rfloor\le n(d)\). After discarding finitely many initial dimensions, which does not affect limits, retain deterministically the first \(\lfloor\alpha_0d^2\rfloor\) observations. By antitone monotonicity (Section 2), \(n_1\le n_2\Rightarrow \mathcal F_{n_2}\subseteq\mathcal F_{n_1}\), so \(\mathcal F_{n(d)}\subseteq \mathcal F_{\lfloor\alpha_0d^2\rfloor}\); hence infeasibility with \(\lfloor\alpha_0d^2\rfloor\) observations implies infeasibility with \(n(d)\) observations.

This proves Theorem 1.1(ii) for unbounded aspect from Theorem 16.4.

QED.

17 Conclusion

Human

Part I constructs an exact positive definite fit whenever the aspect ratio stays a fixed distance below \(1/4\). Part II proves that the centered kernel contains no nonzero positive semidefinite matrix whenever the aspect ratio stays a fixed distance above \(1/4\). These are precisely the two assertions of Theorem 1.1. The proof contains no statement about the critical sequence \(n/d^2\to1/4\).
